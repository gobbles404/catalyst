# Agricultural futures: contract terms for a general trade representation

Reviewed 14 September 2026. Companion to the [petroleum](petroleum-futures-contracts.md), [gas/NGL](natural-gas-and-ngl-futures-contracts.md), [power](power-futures-contracts.md) and [metals](metals-futures-contracts.md) surveys. Scope is exchange-listed futures, with representative global coverage. This is research for the eventual trade template, not an implemented constructor specification.

Agriculture mainly expands **quality and condition**, **quantity measurement**, **delivery documents and logistics**, and **crop/price-reference periods**. Physical and cash settlement occur across the same commodity families. A contract called milk, cattle, coffee or rubber does not establish its unit, delivery obligation or price reference.

## Groups to cover

| Group | Representative commodities |
|---|---|
| Grains and cereals | Corn/maize, different wheat classes, oats and rough rice. |
| Oilseeds and products | Soybeans, soybean meal/oil, rapeseed/canola, palm oil and regional oilseed products. |
| Soft commodities and fibres | Arabica/robusta coffee, cocoa, raw/white sugar, cotton and orange juice. |
| Livestock and animal products | Live and feeder cattle, hogs, pork cutout and eggs. |
| Dairy | Milk classes, milk powder, butter, cheese, whey, milk fat and farmgate milk price. |
| Wider agricultural materials and foods | Lumber, natural rubber, spices and aquaculture. These are included as adjacent exchange-listed agricultural exposures. |

The main venues reviewed are CME/CBOT, MIAX Futures (formerly MGEX), ICE, Euronext, Bursa Malaysia Derivatives, SGX, Osaka Exchange, DCE, ZCE, SGX-NZX, EEX, B3, NCDEX and JSE. A quoted spot index, an OTC supply agreement or a commercial forward is not included merely because it can be cleared. Options, crop insurance, weather derivatives and agricultural equities are outside this review. Biofuel contracts overlap the petroleum review; an agricultural feedstock does not by itself make a fuel contract a new agricultural structure.

`M` means the named contract month; `t` means metric tonne; `lb` means pound; `cwt` below means the U.S. hundredweight of 100 lb. A short ton is 2,000 lb. Bushel mass equivalents are commodity-specific contractual conversions. Milk solids, juice solids, conditioned cotton weight and live/carcass weight are distinct measurement bases. Ticks below are quoted-price increments unless stated otherwise.

## CME/CBOT and U.S. grain exchanges

The U.S. contracts illustrate physical certificates, animal delivery, cash indexes and publisher-announcement settlement. Where a grain row says **standard expiry**, trading ends on the business day preceding the 15th of M. Delivery completion has its own rule and is not automatically the expiry date.

CME's own conversion guide uses **56 lb per corn bushel, 60 lb per soybean/wheat bushel and 38 lb per oats futures bushel**. Do not import a generic 32-lb agricultural oats convention or confuse the contract conversion with a separately measured quality test weight. For milk, a 200,000-lb lot quoted per cwt has a **2,000-unit multiplier**; for Lumber the quote-scale multiplier is **27.5**, before delivery adjustments. [CME metric conversion guide](https://www.cmegroup.com/trading/agricultural/files/AC-225_MetricGuide.pdf)

FCOJ permits **±3% solids quantity**. For juice above 66° Brix, its rule fixes the conversion at **7.278 lb solids/gallon**. Thus concentration, measured volume and contractual solids quantity must not collapse into a generic liquid-mass field. [FCOJ rules 13.02–13.05](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/rulebooks/futures_us/13_FCOJ.pdf)

Age is not one field with one adjustment: cotton has **weight allowances** linked to certification age and separate **price discounts** linked to growth year. Some fibre adjustments use designated market quotations near delivery. Coffee also uses certification/grading age; robusta has stepped monthly age deductions. [Cotton rules 10.22/10.33–10.34](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/rulebooks/futures_us/10_Cotton.pdf), [KC Appendix V](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/rulebooks/futures_us/8_Coffee.pdf), [robusta GGGG.10](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/contractregs/105_SECTION_GGGG.pdf)

Canola's delivery certificate gives a **right to call for shipment**; the subsequent location nomination, shipment quantity, grade and dockage are separate facts. London cocoa's delivery-unit alternatives similarly differ from its trading lot. Sugar No. 11 restricts manufacture age and defines polarization adjustments relative to notice price. These support configurable delivery and invoicing terms rather than a universal nominal-quantity invoice. [Canola delivery rules](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/rulebooks/futures_us/12_Canola_Rules.pdf), [London cocoa rules](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/contractregs/101_SECTION_EEEE.pdf), [SB rules 11.00/11.08](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/rulebooks/futures_us/11_Sugar_11.pdf)

## What agriculture adds to the eventual template

### Quality, condition and measurement

Record the exact commodity form and price basis: grain versus meal versus oil; live animal versus carcass versus meat cutout; liquid milk versus milk solids versus powder. A product's moisture, protein, oil content, test weight, colour, defects, processing grade, origin and crop year can affect eligibility or the invoice. These belong in the applicable contract definition or referenced quality schedule. Actual inspection results belong to the delivered allocation.

Avoid a universal bushel-to-tonne or bale-to-kilogram converter. Use the commodity-specific bushel mass convention and the exchange's bale/conditioned-weight rules. A “bag” may be a price unit rather than a promise of bagged delivery. Likewise, pounds of juice solids do not mean pounds of juice liquid.

Keep four quantities separate: **trading lot, quoted-price denominator, delivery-unit multiple and actual invoice quantity**. The mismatch can require delivery batching. Contractual tolerances, moisture adjustments and rounded multipliers should be preserved rather than regenerated from an assumed standard unit conversion.

### Delivery mechanism and allowed choices

“Physical” can mean transfer of a warehouse receipt, transfer of a shipping certificate, silo-account transfer, shipment on a vessel/railcar, or delivery of animals to an approved point. Documents, transport and load-out are distinct events. The contract needs its permitted delivery method, locations, timing, grade alternatives, premiums/discounts and the party entitled to make each choice.

A quality discount, storage fee or freight/location adjustment is not automatically a separately traded basis future. It may simply be part of the physical invoice. Keep such adjustments separate from a premium/index future's own payoff.

### Contract month, crop season and observation period

Store the named series independently from its last-trade, notice/tender, delivery, observation and payment dates. B3 SOY observes prices before the named month; MKP refers to a production season; salmon uses a week-defined period. A monthly label alone cannot generate these schedules.

Separate index observation dates from publication dates and final-price determination. Preserve aggregation order, weights, selected auction product or reference-future maturity, missing-publication rules and final rounding. An index can describe detailed physical goods while the future settles entirely in cash.

### Futures exposure versus settlement obligations

The basic position still has a signed number of contracts, an execution price and a resolved listed contract. Its cash movement follows that contract's multiplier and settlement treatment. Physical deliverability choices affect the quoted instrument and eventual obligations; they do not justify replacing its definition with a generic fixed-date spot forward.

Final index settlement, daily variation margin and full physical-delivery invoicing are separate cash-flow events. Once daily variation has been settled, computing the same trade-to-final gain again would double-count it. Pricing, forecasting and calculation stay outside the trade record, consistent with [Principle 7](../../PRINCIPLES.md).

A **crush spread** is an execution/package concept unless a separate listed product specifies otherwise. CME's Board Crush consists of 10 soybean, 11 soybean-meal and 9 soybean-oil contracts; preserve those underlying positions and their units. A screen spread price is not enough to define a new one-leg physical obligation. [CME Board Crush](https://www.cmegroup.com/trading/agricultural/grain-and-oilseed/soybean-crush-spreads.html)

Oilshare is different: it is a standalone future on a **ratio index**. Its changes in value are linear in its own quoted index, even though that index is not a linear combination of oil and meal prices. The contract can belong to the futures family while its reference-price definition carries the ratio. [Oilshare rules](https://www.cmegroup.com/rulebook/CBOT/II/12D.pdf)

- **U.S. delivery versions:** MIAX's shipping-certificate migration applies from September 2026 contracts, and CME Live Cattle contains distinct delivery procedures beginning October 2026. Apply the rule by contract series, not simply the date the file was retrieved. [MIAX transition notice](https://www.miaxglobal.com/alert/2025/09/29/miax-futures-exchange-minneapolis-hard-red-spring-wheat-migration-shipping)
- **Lean Hog expiry:** The retrieved futures chapter did not state a general last-trading-day clause. Regular-cycle options rules corroborate the usual tenth-business-day expiry, but obtain the actual futures expiry from CME's current expiration calendar before onboarding. [CME options specifications](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/agriculture/livestock/lean-hogs/specs/options?videoId=6392882945112)
- **ICE transition and maturity-specific rules:** Coffee C Metric starts with May 2028 delivery while KC ends with March 2028. Some current specifications also announce later-origin, location or quality-rule changes; apply them to the specified delivery months. A future-dated series may already be listed even though delivery is years away. [Coffee transition notice](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/futures_us/exchange_notices/ICE_Futures_US_Exchange_Notice_-_Announcing_Coffee_C_Metric_Contract_20250730.pdf)
- **Euronext delivery wording conflicts:** EBM/EMA summary pages describe a post-15th delivery period, while their linked technical rules put silo risk transfer by the 15th and ownership on full payment. Preserve separate events and resolve the summary discrepancy before implementing a delivery schedule; do not equate either phrase with physical load-out. The French wheat rule confirms the technical wording. - **Asian amendment/access gaps:** Bursa terms were recovered from official brochures and December 2024 schedules; ZCE CF/PM use 2024 rules and DCE LH a 2025 revision. SICOM's 20.16-t TSR20 delivery unit is published, but its exact allocation/invoice treatment was not recovered.
- **EEX dairy remains listed during a planned transition.** On 8 September 2026 EEX announced immediate limits on the latest tradable maturities: June 2027 for butter, whey and liquid milk, and December 2027 for skimmed milk powder. The planned transition to CME is subject to closing conditions; it does not establish an already-live replacement contract.
