# Freight futures: contract terms for a general trade representation

Reviewed 14 September 2026. Companion to the commodity-family surveys in this directory. Scope is exchange-listed futures, including futures admitted through exchange trade registration. The objective is representative contractual coverage for a general futures trade object; this is not a security master or an implemented constructor specification.

**Freight adds service-rate and index exposures to the commodity universe.** A contract can represent one charter day, 1,000 tonnes of freight, one or five container equivalents, or a fixed currency amount per index point. The physical size of the benchmark ship is a different fact from the futures lot. All representative contracts below settle financially: holding them does not book a vessel, obtain a container slot, or deliver the cargo.

## Groups and boundaries

| Group | Underlying distinction | Representative coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Dry bulk time charter | Daily hire rate for a specified vessel, route or weighted route basket | Capesize, Panamax, Supramax and Handysize; ICE, SGX and EEX. |
| Dry bulk voyage freight | Cost per tonne on a directional voyage | Brazil–China, Western Australia–China and Colombia–Europe routes. |
| Tanker freight | Clean products versus dirty/crude cargoes; route, vessel and cost conventions | Baltic and Platts assessments; monthly, daily mini and balance-of-month futures. |
| LNG freight | Charter-rate assessment with vessel technology and voyage assumptions | Baltic BLNG-174 and Spark25S/30S. |
| LPG freight | Freight rate per tonne for a defined gas-cargo route | Baltic BLPG1 and BLPG3. |
| Container freight | Directional lane, container class and benchmark methodology | FBX, NYFI, XSI-C and SCFIS Europe. |
| Freight indexes | A scaled basket index instead of a single service rate | ICE Baltic Dry Index future. |

“FFA” is not a sufficient instrument-type test. SGX explicitly lists separate **swaps and futures** for the same dry-freight benchmarks; its futures and corresponding swaps can share prices and expiry dates. This survey includes the futures columns. An ICE product called an “FFA Future” is an exchange future. EEX's trade-registration futures also belong here, even though execution is negotiated before registration. An uncleared bilateral FFA does not. [SGX product comparison](https://www.sgx.com/derivatives/products/ffa-futures?cc=HV), [EEX registration rules](https://www.eex.com/en/trading-resources/trading-information/rules-and-regulations)

`M` denotes the contract month. Prices and tick values below are in USD unless stated otherwise. `t` is a metric tonne; `FEU` is a forty-foot equivalent unit. Tick values shown by multiplication are arithmetic consequences of the quoted increment and lot, not additional exchange charges. Publication calendars, exchange calendars and clearing/payment calendars are distinct.

Select the futures, rather than adjacent FFA, identifiers. Current page establishes product identities and units; exact holiday and payment configurations require the individual specification/calendar. [SGX comparison](https://www.sgx.com/derivatives/products/ffa-futures?cc=HV) |
| EEX dry voyage routes — **C3EM / C5EM / C7EM** | **1,000 t**; USD/t; tick USD 0.01 = USD 10/lot | Cash monthly Baltic averages for Tubarao–Qingdao, Western Australia–Qingdao and Bolivar–Rotterdam. Last registration month-end, with 24 December/preceding exchange-day exception. [Rules 1.4](https://www.eex.com/fileadmin/EEX/Downloads/Rules/EEX-Contract_Specifications_for_Trade_Registration/20260723_EEX_Contract_Specifications_for_Trade_Registration_047a_EN_FINAL.pdf) |
| ICE Baltic Dry Index — **BDI** | **USD 10 per index point**; quote index points; tick 1 point = USD 10/lot | Cash average of published BDI observations. Month-end expiry, December 24/preceding business day exception; payment two clearing business days after the determination period's last day. The contract multiplier is monetary, not a ship-day or tonne quantity. [Specification](https://www.ice.com/products/83048584/Baltic-Dry-Index-BDI-Future) |

EEX additionally lists one-day dry time-charter baskets and individual Panamax trip routes, with USD 1/day ticks. Its longer-period registrations are **strips of consecutive monthly contracts**, including quarterly and calendar packages. This does not establish a separately averaged quarter payoff or a power-style cascading instrument. [EEX dry-freight catalogue](https://www.eex.com/en/global-commodities)

For the EEX contracts reviewed, final cash fulfilment follows last registration on the next ECC business day that is also an eligible USD settlement day at ECC's correspondent bank. The final adjustment uses the previous settlement price and terminal index; it should not be added again as a fresh inception-to-expiry profit after daily variation has already been booked. [EEX fulfilment rules, 1.4–1.5](https://www.eex.com/fileadmin/EEX/Downloads/Rules/EEX-Contract_Specifications_for_Trade_Registration/20260723_EEX_Contract_Specifications_for_Trade_Registration_047a_EN_FINAL.pdf)

The BDI page has a residual reference to USD/day inside its final-settlement prose, despite explicitly specifying index-point quotation and a USD 10 multiplier. This is a source inconsistency, not a reason to assign the future a charter-day unit. Cross-check the governing specification when promoting research into reference data.

## Tanker freight

| Representative futures | Lot / quotation / tick | Settlement and defining terms |
|---|---|---|
| ICE Baltic **TD3C / TDL** and NYMEX Platts **TD3C / TD3** | Both **1,000 t**; USD/t; tick USD 0.0001 = USD 0.10/lot | Both cash, but distinct publisher/formula. ICE averages Baltic route assessments and has the December 24 exception. NYMEX averages daily Platts Middle East Gulf–China 270,000-t Worldscale assessments after applying that day's flat rate; its period is the **full month, including December**, and last trade is month's last business day. [ICE](https://www.ice.com/products/57250507/TD3C-FFA-Middle-East-Gulf-to-China-Baltic-Future), [NYMEX chapter 871](https://www.cmegroup.com/rulebook/NYMEX/8/871.pdf) |
| NYMEX West Coast Canada–China **TD28** | **1,000 t**; USD/t; tick USD 0.0001 = USD 0.10/lot | Cash Baltic Vancouver–Ningbo benchmark for 80,000-t cargoes. Each published **voyage lump sum is divided by benchmark cargo size** before averaging. January–November full month; December 1–24. Last trade on final index-publication day in that period, adjusted if not an exchange business day. [Chapter 702](https://www.cmegroup.com/rulebook/NYMEX/7/702.pdf) |
| ICE Daily TD3C Mini — **DFN** | **100 t**; USD/t; tick USD 0.0001 = USD 0.01/lot | Cash daily determination period. Last trade is the **second business day after nominal contract date**; payment another two clearing business days later. If the period has no publication day, the last preceding publication supplies the fallback. [Daily specification](https://www.ice.com/products/67119990/Daily-TD3C-FFA-Middle-East-Gulf-to-China-%28Baltic%29-Mini-Future) |
| ICE TC5 Platts balance-of-month — **WNX** | **1,000 t**; USD/t; tick USD 0.0001 = USD 0.10/lot | Cash remainder-period exposure to 55,000-t Arabian Gulf–Japan clean freight, using Platts Worldscale assessments and TC5 flat rate. Last trade month's last trading day; payment +2 clearing business days. Page does not fully define how the chosen start date is encoded: retain the actual determination period. [Specification](https://www.ice.com/products/32375872/TC5-FFA-Arabian-Gulf-to-Japan-Platts-Balmo-Future) |

Worldscale is a relative rate: **WS100 is 100% of the published flat rate**. For a feed expressing the assessment as 150 points, the conventional normalized factor is 150/100. A contract referring to Worldscale can nevertheless trade and settle in USD/t. Store the input's scale and the contract's conversion rule; applying an already normalized factor as raw points creates a hundredfold error. The Platts TD3C rule also applies the relevant flat rate **daily before averaging**. A single annual flat-rate multiplier is equivalent only when that rate is unchanged throughout the selected period. [Worldscale's explanation](https://www.worldscale.co.uk/company-history), [NYMEX conversion rule](https://www.cmegroup.com/rulebook/NYMEX/8/871.pdf)

Coverage extends beyond the representative rows: ICE's current freight rules contain clean TC2/TC5/TC6/TC14 and other regional routes, dirty TD7/TD8/TD19/TD20/TD22/TD25/TD28, and Platts alternatives. They also distinguish **US Gulf–UK Continent freight excluding EU ETS** from the other specification. Carbon-cost treatment can therefore be part of benchmark identity, even within the same lane. [ICE current Part II E contents and contracts](https://www.ice.com/publicdocs/contractregs/86_SECTION_TTT_Part_II_E.pdf)

Spark's methodology makes vessel technology, capacity, fuel and boil-off assumptions material to the benchmark. Its USD/day rate is distinct from its calculated USD/MMBtu transport-cost assessment; the latter includes additional voyage costs and discharge-volume calculations. Do not substitute one because both are labelled “LNG freight.” The contractual benchmark can carry these detailed assumptions by versioned reference rather than reproducing them as user-entered fields on every futures trade. [Spark methodology](https://static.sparkcommodities.com/documents/lng-freight/methodology/Spark-LNG-Freight-Methodology-3.8.pdf)

ICE also lists Spark balance-of-month futures **SPA/SPT**. The Atlantic page distinguishes “Today” and “Next Day” starts, as strips of at least two charter days. Its displayed last-trading rule, however, refers to two UK business days before the contract month's start, which does not reconcile on its face with the stated in-month starts. These products are included as a documented timing exception requiring clarification before configuration. [SPA specification](https://www.ice.com/products/83049430/Spark30S-Atlantic-Sabine-Pass-to-Gate-LNG-Freight-Balmo-Futures), [current LNG catalogue](https://www.ice.com/products/Futures-Options/Energy/Liquified-Natural-Gas)

For INE, the three observations are the last-trading-day index and the preceding two publication dates. Its rules provide publication-delay deadlines and an exchange determination fallback. A missing observation cannot silently become a zero or be carried forward under an invented generic rule. The May 2026 listed-month schedule also differs from older launch materials; retrieve actual admitted maturities instead of assuming every calendar month is available.

These container benchmarks should remain separate even when their geographic descriptions overlap. FBX, NYFI, XSI-C and SCFIS are different named observations. The INE multiplier also creates RMB exposure to index points, not a fixed number of FEUs or a simple currency-converted FBX contract.

Euronext's technical notice provides a practical route to resolving dated observation windows: its derivatives standing-data files include **Start Observation Date** and **End Observation Date**. Those fields should be reconciled with the governing product terms when loading a specific maturity. [Euronext standing-data changes](https://www.euronext.com/en/media/14394/download)

## Current status and source discrepancies

Published specifications are evidence of contract structure; they do not establish available liquidity. For historical-looking families, search notices as well as product pages:

- **NYMEX's older LNG BL1/BL2/BL3 were delisted on 8 September 2025.** The current BG1/BG2/BG3 174-series is separate. Chapter 702 now describes TD28 tanker freight, so a chapter number alone is not a permanent product identifier. [Delisting notice](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/notices/ser/2025/09/ser-9607.pdf), [current chapter 702](https://www.cmegroup.com/rulebook/NYMEX/7/702.pdf)
- **NYMEX BLPG1 BALMO FLB was suspended in March 2026**, with additional listing ceased. This is not the same status as the monthly FLP future above. [SER 9700](https://www.cmegroup.com/content/dam/cmegroup/notices/ser/2026/03/ser-9700.pdf)
- EEX identifies transitions away from older Capesize and Supramax baskets. Its summary gives both December 2028 as Supramax 10TC's last maturity and January 2027 as a delisting date; that transition needs the detailed notice before booking distant maturities. [Current EEX catalogue](https://www.eex.com/en/global-commodities)
- The 2022 CME container factsheet says month-end expiry; current chapter 427 specifies publication-day expiry and December truncation. Current rules take precedence. The ICE dry-freight, Spark BALMO and Euronext inconsistencies identified above remain explicit configuration gaps.
- Nodal's trucking futures are historical: its 2020 filing delisted all eleven. This review did not establish a current exchange-listed air, rail or trucking future to add. Shipping equities, freight-price indexes without futures, and freight-related options are outside the inventory. [Nodal delisting filing](https://www.cftc.gov/IndustryOversight/IndustryFilings/PTCDCMRules/44403)

## Consequences for the eventual trade template

These are design inferences from the contractual examples, not a proposed schema implementation.

1. **Keep quantity and benchmark scale independent.** A 174,000-m³ LNG reference vessel can underpin a one-day futures lot. A 270,000-t tanker assessment can underpin a 1,000-t lot. Index-point multipliers need their own monetary dimension.
2. **Identify the benchmark precisely.** Publisher, route direction, vessel/basket version, cargo or container class, cost inclusions and assessment currency determine which observation settles the trade. A generic location pair is insufficient.
3. **Represent observation schedules explicitly.** Full-month publication averages, December truncation, daily contracts, balance-of-month starts and last-three-publication sampling cannot all be reconstructed from expiry month alone.
4. **Represent contractual transformations.** Examples include Worldscale normalization and applicable flat rates, voyage lump sum divided by benchmark tonnes, and index points multiplied by currency per point. A fixed monetary multiplier is not a fixed notional amount.
5. **Separate final fixing, last trade and cash payment.** Each may use a different calendar. Keep daily variation settlement distinct from the final residual adjustment. For intermediated contracts, client cash timing need not equal CCP timing.
6. **Distinguish a listed spread or index from an execution package.** A strip of monthly futures retains constituent expiries and quantities. A route-basket index is one contractual reference. Any true differential future needs its specified components, signs, conversion and observation alignment; two selected legs are not automatically one such instrument.
7. **Version contract facts.** Preserve exchange product identity, effective rules and benchmark changes. Published old futures, replacement vessels and suspended maturities must not collapse into a single mutable “freight” definition.

For example, **30 one-day lots** exposed to a USD 100/day rate change produce USD 3,000 of undiscounted variation, before rounding or fees. The monthly label selects an observation period; it does not automatically multiply those 30 lots by another 30 days. Similarly, one INE EC lot moving 100 index points changes by RMB 5,000 without requiring a fictitious container quantity. These simple unit checks are useful acceptance examples when the common trade representation is designed.
