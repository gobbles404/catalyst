# The numpy code at main, reviewed for optimizations

A review of every numpy and scipy call in the library as it stands at the merge of #118, the tree pull request #119 was opened against, asking of each one whether the work is needed at all, whether it was already done, and only then whether it can run faster: the order the tenets set. Every claim below was measured on this tree, on the whole prepared path a formula runs, and every proposed change was implemented in a worktree, proved equivalent on the success path and on every failure path, run against the unit tests and the budget pins, and then handed to two reviewers whose job was to refute it. The harness that produced the numbers is `tools/performance/numpy_review/`, the per-candidate diffs are under `docs/reviews/numpy-review/patches/`, and the reviewers' tables and notes are under `docs/reviews/numpy-review/evidence/`.

Nothing in the library's source changes in this pull request. The findings are proposals, each with its counts before and after, for the owner to take one at a time; the ones worth taking are few and named first.

## How to read a number

Counts before time, as `docs/seam/measuring.md` asks. For each finding the evidence is, in this order: the peak of the Python heap during one warm call as `tracemalloc` sees it (numpy's data buffers included); the ledger of numpy C calls and of the package's own functions as `tests/performance/conftest.py` counts them; the passes over the data, read off the code and checked against the ledgers; and last the clock. Timings were taken with the machine shared by the review's own agents, so a move under about 15% is noise unless it is consistent across sizes; the compare tables mark a move "real" only when the two sides' spreads across three to five rounds do not overlap. The sizes are the repository's: 100 cells is the working size and 10,000 the control; a ten-year daily curve is 3,631 days; a futures calendar 360, 3,630 and 36,000 days; fixings 260, 2,600 and 26,000 rows; schedules 120 and 1,200 contracts.

Two things to know about the instruments. The pinned numpy ledger misses two C functions, `count_nonzero` and `interp`, because its pattern reads only undotted names (rank 16 below); every ledger difference in this document is from the pinned pattern, so those two never appear in a "before" or an "after", and neither tree is favoured. And the harness takes a creator's peak as the least of three released calls, as `tests/performance` takes a reader's, because a one-call peak can absorb a rebuild of the handle store's dictionary (the rejected instrument candidate under "Instruments" shows it: 1,144 B most calls, 6,737 B when the rebuild lands).

## What the tree is before any change

At 40fab53 every check passes: ruff format and check, pyright strict with no warnings, and 1,376 tests in twelve seconds. The repository's own runner, `tools/performance/measure_python.py`, and the review's harness reproduce the pinned ceilings to within a few hundred bytes: the daily curve creator peaks at 216,459 bytes against the pin's 216,443, the table probe at 135,184 against 135,266, the mixed road out at exactly 50,536. The harness adds what the runner lacks, rows for every creator and reader at the sizes above, with a creator's publication released inside the clock so the store never grows, which #114's `release` made possible.

The noise floor of the harness, the as-is tree compared against itself in interleaved fresh processes, is in `evidence/noise_floor.md`: nine rows, one of them "moved" by 11% with no code change, the rest inside their spreads, every peak and ledger identical. That is the bar a clock move has to clear.

## Method

1. Every numpy and scipy call site in `src/goosedove` was read by finders working in ten groups (the seam's way in, its way out twice, the Excel utilities, the calendar, the fixings, the market history, the interpolation, the definitions, and the measuring instruments themselves). They raised 91 findings, merged by hand and by a second pass into 53 candidates with no overlap.
2. Each candidate was implemented as its own commit in a worktree off 40fab53, by a verifier who had to prove it before recommending it: an equivalence script running the as-is tree and the worktree in separate processes with warnings as errors, comparing array bytes, dtype, shape, contiguity, the writeable flag and every exception's type and message word for word; the unit tests; the budget pins, with every moved pin listed with its new value; and the harness, three to five rounds, on the scenarios the change touches plus controls it must not.
3. Two refuters then attacked each recommended candidate independently, one on equivalence (inputs the verifier did not try, Python-only inputs included) and one on the house rules (`CLAUDE.md`, `PRINCIPLES.md`, the golden rules, and what `history.md` already declined or parked). A refuter who found a flaw wrote the reproducing script and said what it broke.
4. Four small fixes repaired the five candidates the refuters broke on a Python-only input; each was re-proved against the refuters' own scripts and the verifier's suite, re-run against the pins and the harness, and is reported with the fixed numbers (the fix patches are the last file in each group's directory, and `evidence/<group>/NOTES-fix.md` says what was done). The rest of the refuted candidates are under "Rejected" with the refuters' numbers.

Reproduction: `bench_compare.py --proposed <tree> --scenarios <names> --rounds 5` prints the tables under `evidence/`; `bench_harness.py --list` names the scenarios. The verifiers' scripts are referenced by name in each `evidence/<group>/NOTES.md`.

## Recommended, by area

Each entry says what changes, why it is less work and not merely faster work, the counts before and after, the pins that move, what the refuters tried, and the one caveat the pull request should state. The diff is `patches/<group>/<nn>-<id>.patch`, and the full tables are `evidence/<group>/<id>.md`.

### The seam, the way in

**A table's column of text is the host's tuple sliced** (`bridge-table-text-column`, patch `bridge-in/02`). Today `_refine` finds a table's text column by a whole-body `is_text` mask, a per-row sum, a cumsum, an object array of the texts, a fancy gather and a rebuilt tuple, with a cached helper to share the positions across columns. The k-th text cell of a body is the k-th text, so with T text-bound columns the column at rank t among them is `texts[t::T]`, one tuple slice, no numpy at all. Counts: `SumQuantity` at 10,000 cells, peak 135,184 to 43,940 B (13.5 to 4.4 bytes per cell); `_refine` alone on the Trades table 15,798 to 5,554 B at 250 rows; `_CreateContractSpecifications` 13,080 to 12,904. The table-in numpy ledger goes `numpy.array` 1 to 0, `cumsum` 1 to 0, `sum` 3 to 1, `ufunc.reduce` 7 to 5, and the package ledger loses `_refine.<locals>.<lambda>` and `_text_places`. Clock: `probe.SumQuantity.10k` 330 to 101 µs, real; `create.ContractSpecifications.120` 122 to 71 µs, real. Pins: three ledgers in `test_seam_budgets.py`. Equivalence: 713 tables over ten schemas with a bad cell of every kind at the first, middle and last row, 0 differ; both refuters stood it at high confidence. Caveat: the slice rests on a cross-column invariant, that every other column type refuses a text cell (number and boolean by kind, date by its NaN slot), stated in the comment. The pull request should add a unit test that each type in the column table refuses a text cell, so a future mixed column cannot break the slice silently.

**Dates in** (`bridge-date-column-in`, patch `bridge-in/01`). Today a date column is checked by a floor, two compares, an `or` and an `any`, with a kinds mask `or`-ed in, and converted by two `astype` calls through a timedelta temporary: 17 bytes per cell at the peak. The proposal floors once and asks three reductions (the floor's min is at least 61, its max at most 2,958,465, and the floor equals the slots), builds no mask on the success path, and converts in the floor's own buffer: shift by the epoch, cast float to int64 in place with `copyto` over 1-D views (numpy guarantees `copyto` on overlapping operands; C or F layout is kept), then view as days. A rejected range drops the floor and builds today's mask to name the cell. Counts: `FirstOfDates` 100 cells 2,892 to 1,408 B, 10,000 cells 171,192 to 90,449 B (9 bytes per cell); the holiday-calendar creator at 100 holidays 2,892 to 2,208; schedules unchanged (the library's own work sets those peaks). Per date column the numpy ledger loses two `astype` and one `any` and gains a `min`, a `max`, an `all`, two `ravel` and two `view`; the package ledger swaps `_not_dates` for `_whole_serials` on the success path. Clock: `look.FirstOfDates.10k` 113 to 40 µs, real; at 100 cells inside the spread; the rejected-fraction control at 10,000 cells 47 to 64 µs in one run and 29 to 51 (+73%, real) in the combined run: a rejected range now pays one more floor and min pass, a real regression on the failure path. Pins: 12 ledgers. Equivalence: every serial from 61 to 2,958,465 through `_dates` in six layouts, 20,000 random ranges over a 20-value pool (70,002 outcomes), 394 named cases over 25 edge values, 0 differ. The rules refuter noted this is the parked history item and left the call to the owner; the clock the parked item asked for is now the `look.FirstOfDates` row.

**A scalar date in** (`bridge-scalar-date-in`, patch `bridge-in/03`). `_date` builds `EXCEL_EPOCH + np.timedelta64(int(serial), "D")`: a timedelta construction and a scalar add. `np.datetime64(int(serial) + EPOCH_DAYS, "D")` is one construction. Exhaustive over the 2,958,405 serials: identical value, dtype, type and int64 payload. Counts: `_date` 984 to 64 B; `_CreateValuationDate` 1,144 to 272 B; `FirstOfDates` on one cell 1,144 to 568. No ledger sees a numpy scalar constructor, so no pin moves. Clock: `_date` 2.49 to 0.71 µs; `create.ValuationDate` 7.29 to 4.28 µs, inside the spread, and 6.73 to 4.48 on the micro clock with release, spreads apart. No pin moves, so the pull request should lower a ceiling to claim the bytes. This is the way-in half of the parked "scalar date path".

**A table column's kind look as reductions** (`bridge-kind-column-looks-as-reductions`, patch `bridge-in/04`) is equivalent and saves a byte per row per kind column, but the rules refuter downgraded it to marginal: at 25 rows the text and boolean looks cost 3.08 µs against 1.78 today, the table-in numpy ledger rises by two calls, and the only peak move is 2,500 B at the 10,000-cell control. It is listed under "Marginal".

### The seam, the way out

**Dates out, and the table's date column written into its slots** (`bridge-serials-out` and `bridge-table-column-writes-into-slots`, patches `bridge-out-a/01` and `02`, repaired). Today `_serials` runs `isnat` and `any`, a timedelta subtraction, a true divide, two compares, an `or` and a second `any`: eight passes and three full-size temporaries per date column, and `_table_column` then builds a serials array and copies it into the strided slot column, a second write of the body. The proposal reads the days through their int64 view (a view, not a copy); NaT is the int64 minimum, so `min` and `max` settle the NaT look and the calendar look together, with the mask built only to name the failing cell; the serials are one cast to float64 shifted in place, and the table's date column is cast straight into its strided slot column and shifted there.

The refuters broke the committed form: the gate `np.datetime_data(dtype)[0] == "D"` admits `datetime64[2D]`, `[7D]` and big-endian `>M8[D]` arrays, which only a Python caller can build, and for those the int64 view is not a count of days. A `[2D]` column came out as the wrong serials with no error (as-is 46,163 and 46,165, proposed 35,866 and 35,867) and a big-endian column was refused as "outside the calendar". The fix is one line at the gate: `days.astype(DAY, copy=False).view(np.int64)` with `DAY` the native day dtype. `ndarray.astype` with `copy=False` returns the array itself when no cast is needed, proven on a native, a read-only and a strided day column (`x.astype(DAY, copy=False) is x`), so a library record's column costs nothing; a `[2D]` or big-endian array is cast to native days first and then gives exactly the as-is serials. The fix is commit 228f94c in the worktree, patch `bridge-out-a/06`: a `DAY` constant and the cast in both places the view is taken, the gate unchanged, the docstrings saying the view is over DAY. Re-proved: the refuters' three scripts give byte-identical output against as-is, and an extended probe of `[2D]`, `[7D]` and `[D]` in both byte orders, each with an in-range day, a NaT, a day outside the calendar on either side, an int64 extreme and a strided layout, through the column, row, table and scalar roads, is 168 same and 0 different with warnings as errors. The scalar `_date_out` was wrong the same way before the fix and is right after it, since it runs through `_serials`. The no-copy claim was proved on a frozen record column of 14,286 days (`col.astype(DAY, copy=False) is col`, and the table's peak is exactly its slots plus its kinds, 129,348 B, against 372,714 as-is). The verifier's four suites re-run on the fixed tree give the same totals as before the fix. The fix's whole cost on the counts is `ndarray.astype` +1 per date column on the numpy ledger (`EchoDates.10k` 3 to 4, `FuturesSchedule.120` 0 to 2, the readers 0 to 1); every peak is identical in all 21 scenarios.

Counts, as-is to proposed with both: `EchoDates` 10,000 251,256 to 171,192 B; the curve reader 128,381 to 102,524; the holiday-calendar reader 3,850 to 2,212; a date column out alone 62,631 to 29,480 B at 3,631 days; the curve table's two columns 128,381 to 66,150 B at 3,631 rows. Per date column the numpy ledger loses two `any` and gains `view`, `min` and `max` (and an `astype` after the fix); the package ledger swaps `_serials` for `_not_days` on the table path. Passes over a date column on success: 8 to 4. Clock: `read.DiscountCurve.3631` 103 to 79 µs, `look.EchoDates.10k` 142 to 86, `read.HolidayCalendar.100` 49 to 40, all inside the spread on a noisy box but consistent across sizes. Pins: six reader ledgers, and the valuation-date reader's ceiling: that reader goes 1,671 to 2,102 B because numpy's in-place ufunc on a one-element array costs about a kilobyte of traced memory, nothing at two elements, so the pin `2 * SLACK` becomes `3 * SLACK`. One Python-only outcome changes and the pull request should say so: the one day `np.datetime64(2**63 - 1, "D")` reached the cell as numpy's own `OverflowError` text and now gets the seam's "is a date outside 1900-03-01 to 9999-12-31".

**The mixed road's three looks** (`bridge-mixed-out-looks`, patch `bridge-out-a/04`). `_mixed_out` builds eleven full-size 1-byte masks across its text count, its "no cell holds nothing" look, its finite look and its boolean look. The proposal reuses two: the `kinds == TEXT` mask is kept, the first look is `kinds.max() > BOOLEAN_CODE` on the success path (the mixed kinds are the three lowest codes, pinned), `_bad_mixed` runs only to name a cell, and the finite and boolean looks are written with `out=` into the two masks. Counts: `EchoMixed` over 10,000 numbers 50,536 to 20,449 B; over a mixed 2,500 × 4 72,213 to 62,117; the 360-day history reader's road out 19,660 to 17,759; the numpy ledger `any` 3 to 1 plus `max` 1 and `all` 1, `ufunc.reduce` unchanged, and `_bad_mixed` leaves the accepted path's package ledger. Clock +23% to -12% across readers, inside the spread. Pins: nine ledgers (the two mixed-out tests and every reader). Equivalence: 4,189 verifier cases, 3,000 of them random records with an injected fault in 70%, of which 4 differ, all off-type records (shapes that broadcast, float kinds holding NaN); then the refuters' 20,000-case sweep over five dtype and layout combinations, 0 differ. **Whole numbers beyond 2^53** (`bridge-beyond-exact-reductions`, patch `bridge-out-b/03`). Counts: the peak is unchanged (a list and a tuple at 8 bytes per text either way); the numpy ledger loses `numpy.array` 1 and `ravel` 1 per table with text columns; the gather alone 126 to 34 µs at 3,631 rows and 1,737 to 638 at 36,630. ### The library

**The dated histories** (`history-dated-binders-slices-and-count`, patch `market-history/01`, repaired). `futures_history` and `gas_daily_history` placed the fixings inside the calendar with full-length masks, a boolean gather and an `astype` copy, and settled coverage with a zeros array over the calendar and a scatter. The proposal finds the inside run by two scalar `searchsorted` calls, takes the rows as one subtraction over that slice, copies the prices from the slice, and settles coverage with one count: the rows are consecutive fixed days exactly when their count equals `first_unfixed`, which rests on four invariants each cited to the builder that establishes it (every inside fixing is priced, the fixing dates strictly increase, the calendar's days are consecutive, `is_fixed` is a prefix). The equivalence refuter found that `fixed_rows` had become a view onto a writeable timedelta temporary (`.base` set, `OWNDATA` false) where as-is it was owned, which the copy-then-freeze rule forbids; the fix is `.astype(np.intp)` over the inside slice in place of `.view`, 8 bytes per inside fixing. The fix is commit 0e739eb, patch `market-history/06`, re-proved on the refuter's 45 cases (`fixed_rows.base` is `None` and `OWNDATA` is set on every success) and the verifier's 9,093. Its cost is 8 bytes per inside fixing on the peak, `least_peak` 1,756 to 1,760 B at 360 and 3,630 days against as-is 3,520 and 16,600, and `ndarray.astype` back to 1 on the ledger where the view had replaced it, so the ledger against as-is is `any` 2 to 0, `zeros` 1 to 0, `ufunc.reduce` 2 to 0, `copy` 0 to 1, `searchsorted` 0 to 2, `astype` 1 unchanged.

**The IFERC history** (`history-iferc-permutation`, patch `market-history/03`). The unfixed rule becomes `rows.max(initial=-1) >= first_unfixed`, coverage is `rows.size != first_unfixed` (it relies on `iferc_fixings` refusing a repeated code, named in the comment), and the output is `fixed_rows = arange(F)` with the prices placed by one inverse scatter instead of an argsort and two gathers. Counts: 120 months 11,520 to 6,488 B, 1,200 months 95,220 to 81,516; the ledger loses `argsort`, `zeros` and two `any` and gains `max`, `arange` and `empty`. Clock -13% to -15%, real at both sizes. Pins: one ledger, whose "no arange" assertion must say the arange is the history's rows, not the calendar's. Equivalence: 3,000 random IFERC calendars with the indices shuffled or in order, every fault, 0 differ.

**Futures fixings, whole by one count** (`futures-fixings-wholeness-by-count`, patch `fixings/02`). The wholeness rule built a full-size `busday_offset` column, a `!=` mask and an `any`. After the order rule and the closed-day rule, which run first in the same function, the N - 1 earlier days are distinct business days inside the span, so the series is whole exactly when `np.busday_count(first, last) == N - 1`: one scalar, no temporary, and the old column is built only to name the row. Counts: 260 rows 11,194 to 8,495 B, 2,600 rows 93,094 to 69,335, 26,000 rows 912,094 to 677,735; `any` 4 to 3, `ufunc.reduce` 4 to 3. Clock -27% to -64%, real at 2,600 and 26,000 rows. Pins: one ledger, and the fixings ceiling lowered 45 to 35 bytes per row. Equivalence: 13,950 cases, 0 mismatches; the refuter tried an empty series, column shapes, 1899 and 9999, days near the int64 ends and a 2^62-day span. The rules refuter objected to the committed `days.size > 1 and ...` guard as a singleton branch (one row needs no guard, since `busday_count(d, d)` is 0). A guard-free form exists and was checked here: `(np.busday_count(days[:1], days[-1:], busdaycal=calendar) != days.size - 1).any()` is `False` on an empty series, on one row and on a whole series, and `True` on a gap, with no branch; the pull request should take that form. With it the ledger pin does not move (`any` stays at 4, since the `.any()` is the call the branch saved; the rules refuter measured it) and only the ceiling does.

**Gas Daily fixings, consecutive by a span** (`gas-daily-consecutive-by-span`, patch `fixings/04`, repaired). The consecutive rule built `days[:-1] + 1 day`, a `!=` mask and an `any`. Days are consecutive exactly when their int64 day numbers strictly increase and the last minus the first is N - 1, so the proposal views the column as int64, makes one `<=` order mask and takes the span as two Python ints, with the old column built only to name the row. Both refuters found the one hole: NaT is the int64 minimum, so a NaT in row 1 followed by the least possible day number passes both tests, and a record with a NaT flow day was built where as-is refuses it. The fix (commit 686f217, patch `fixings/06`) is one scalar test on the first day's number folded into the failure condition, so the failure path is the old code and the message the old words; the refuters' inputs now match as-is, and seven cases were added to the verifier's suite, which catches the hole on the pre-fix tree (4 mismatches) and passes on the fixed one (13,957 cases, 0). Counts after the fix, unchanged from before it: 365 days 13,084 to 10,012 B, 3,652 days 121,555 to 92,187, 36,500 days 1,205,539 to 913,387; `ndarray.view` 0 to 1 is the only ledger move. Clock -25% to -33%. Pins: one ledger, and the ceiling 40 to 30 bytes per day. The same `days.size > 1` guard is in this diff; the pull request should use the branch-free form above or state why a guard is kept. Left as found, Python-only: as-is accepts a (3, 1) column and the candidate raises `TypeError` from `int()` of a row, a loud failure where there was an acceptance.

**The calendar's contract column** (`calendar-contract-by-repeat`, patch `calendar/01`). The futures and Gas Daily calendars placed each day's contract with `searchsorted` over the expiries for every day, O(N log M). `np.repeat(np.arange(1, M), gaps)` over the gaps between expiries is O(N) with two O(M) passes, and the counts are passed inline so the M-length temporaries are freed before the flags are built (a named local raised the peak by 448 B at 360 days and 10,112 at 36,630). Counts: peak identical at every size; the ledger swaps `searchsorted` for `repeat`, a second `arange` and a `view`. Clock: `create.Calendar.futures.3630` 148 to 102 µs, real; at 360 days inside the spread. Pins: two ledgers. Equivalence: 868 schedules × 11 valuation days, 9,548 calls, 9,526 identical and 22 differing, all of them the Python-only case next. Python-only: a NaT in the middle of `future_expiry` passes the order rule today, since NaT compares false, and `searchsorted` then returns a silently wrong contract column; with the change it raises numpy's own error. That is a correctness note in its own right, below.

**The interpolation hold** (`interp-hold-as-reverse-cummax`, patch `interpolation/02`). The hold that keeps a segment from dipping below its right anchor was counts by `concatenate`, `diff` and `astype`, an N-sized `repeat` and a `maximum`. `np.maximum.accumulate` over `values[::-1]` in place is the same hold in one pass, with no temporary: on numpy's segment line `fl(s·t + a)` with a non-positive slope is monotone, so the only bound to keep is the right anchor, and a reverse cumulative maximum keeps it. Counts: the hold's own temporaries 8N + 24M bytes to 448 B at every size; the whole-call peak -328 B (it sits inside `np.interp`); the ledger loses `repeat` 1 and `astype` 1 and gains `ufunc.accumulate` 1, the same ledger for a daily table and a dozen anchors, so the one-ledger claim of the two curve tests survives. Clock +1% to -40% across the curve creators, inside the spread; the hold step alone is faster on daily tables and slower on sparse anchors (12 anchors over 3,631 days 10.7 to 14.4 µs). Pins: `CURVE_NUMPY`, shared by two tests. Equivalence: 50,037 in-contract cases, about 10,000 of them underflow draws where the hold changes values, 0 bit mismatches; the refuter's 20,000 curves in four regimes with 1,551 held values, 0 mismatches. Caveat: a middle NaT in a Python-built input passes the builder today and fails only by accident in `np.repeat`; with the hold gone that accident is gone, and the builder should check for NaT (see "Correctness notes"). On two anchors 1,048,574 days apart the whole function is slower, +9% to +10% (15.4 to 17.0 ms), and the hold step alone 2.5 to 4.2 ms.

**The specifications' bounds as reductions** (`specs-bounds-as-reductions`, patch `definitions/03`, repaired) and **the curve's finite look as `all`** (`curve-finite-look-as-all`, patch `definitions/01`), ideally one pull request. The "below one" rule is `lots.min() < 1.0` and each fee rule `np.fmin.reduce(fees, axis=None) < 0.0`, with the 1-byte-per-row mask built only to name the row; `fmin` and not `min` because `min` propagates NaN and would accept a column `[nan, -1.0]` that is refused at row 2 today. The equivalence refuter found that `fmin.reduce` without `axis=None` reduces along the first axis only, so a 2-D Python-only fee array changed outcome; `axis=None` reduces over every element as `(fees < 0).any()` did. The fix is commit 671e9f1, patch `definitions/05`, re-proved on 0-d, 1-D, 2-D, empty and (3, 0) inputs against as-is, the as-is `IndexError` on a 2-D failure included (a flat `argmax` index used as a row, Python-only, left as found). No count changes with the argument: the ledger does not record a ufunc's reduce. The repair agent rated the candidate marginal on the whole-call clock, where the checks are about 2 µs of a builder that runs 500 µs or more; it is kept here on the counts, as a pair with the curve's look. Counts: the builder's peak at 12 contracts 1,920 to 1,620 B (the mask was that size's peak), unchanged at 120 and above; `any` 9 to 6 and `min` 1 on the ledger, `ufunc.reduce` 12 unchanged; passes on the success path 6 to 3 with no mask. The curve's finite look goes from `isfinite`, invert, `any` to `isfinite`, `all` with the inverted mask only on failure, the idiom `bridge._finite` already uses: one pass and one 1-byte mask fewer, `any` 5 to 4 and `all` 1, the whole-call peak unchanged at every size. Both are exactly equivalent on every case tried (144 and 50 named cases, the refuter's 20,000 random curves and his 2-D, 0-d, list, object and str inputs), and both clocks are inside noise; they earn a pull request as "less", not as "faster".

### Instruments

**The ledger pattern drops dotted builtins** (`instrument-ledger-regex-drops-dotted-builtins`, patch `instruments/01`). The numpy ledger's pattern `<built-in method numpy\.(\w+)>` cannot match a function cProfile names by its module path, so `numpy._core._multiarray_umath.count_nonzero` and `..._multiarray_umath.interp` were silently dropped. `<built-in method numpy(?:\.\w+)*\.(\w+)>` keeps the leaf name. Across all 73 harness scenarios, 30 gain an entry and no existing entry changes; 15 pins gain `numpy.count_nonzero: 1` (the mixed road's text count, the rejected table, every reader, the three calendar creators) or `numpy.interp: 1` (the two curve tests). Test-only; it should land first, so every later pin moves against the complete ledger.

**A ledger from `sys.monitoring`** (`instrument-numpy-callables-ledger-sys-monitoring`, patch `instruments/02`). cProfile raises no event for ufuncs called by name, for the C dispatchers behind `is_busday`, `busday_offset`, `interp`, `searchsorted`, `count_nonzero` and `copyto`, or for numpy types built. The tool id is 4, since Hypothesis holds 3 and CPython reserves 0, 1, 2 and 5, and `use_tool_id` fails loudly on a clash. Caveat: which dispatcher a numpy Python function reaches (`np.full` to `copyto`) is numpy's internal.

**What a creator keeps, to the byte** (`instrument-retained-numpy-bytes-by-tracemalloc-domain`, patch `instruments/06`). numpy registers every data buffer in its own `tracemalloc` domain (389047); reading that domain after a creator call and after its release gives exactly the numpy bytes a publication holds, which pins the copy-then-freeze rule directly: the daily curve holds 58,096 B then 0, the fixings 4,160 then 0, the holiday calendar 800 then 0, the calendar 6,480 then 0. **Creator rows in the runner and the profiler** (`instrument-creators-timed-with-release`, patch `instruments/04`), with one condition. `measure_python.py` has no creator rows; the candidate adds 14, each publishing and releasing inside the clock. The rules refuter showed that the runner's one-call `peak_bytes` is nondeterministic for these rows: every insert-then-pop into the handle store uses a slot the pop does not give back, so the dictionary rebuilds about every twenty inserts, the timing pass's calibrated iteration count decides which row's peak takes the rebuild, and `_CreatePriceVector` at 10,000 read 81,065, 81,065 and 80,346 B in three fresh processes. ## Correctness notes found in passing

None of these reaches a worksheet: the seam converts only validated whole-day serials and finite numbers, and every one needs a Python caller. They are recorded because the refuters' scripts reproduce them on the as-is tree, and because several of the rejected candidates above would have changed them silently.

1. **A NaT in the middle of `future_expiry`** passes the futures calendar's order rule, since NaT compares false, and `searchsorted` over the unsorted expiries returns a wrong contract column with no error (row 1 gets 0 days, row 2 gets 60). The same gap exists for an IFERC schedule's publication days. Rank 12 turns it into numpy's own `ValueError` by accident; the builder should refuse NaT by name.
2. **A middle NaT in a `DiscountCurve` input** passes `discount_curve`'s order rule the same way and fails today only because `np.repeat` refuses a negative count; rank 13 removes that accident, so the builder needs a NaT check of its own, in the pull request that lands rank 13 or before it.
3. **An infinite lot size and a NaN fee** pass `contract_specifications` today (inf is its own floor and at least 1; NaN is not below zero). The design fork `specs-inf-lot-and-nan-fee-correctness` is the fix, with its five unit tests.
4. **`valuation_date()` accepts a NaT day and keeps the unit it is given**, so a `datetime64[s]` valuation day is compared against day columns as supplied. Every calendar builder relies on the seam having normalized it. The two rejected calendar candidates above are the ones that would have turned this into wrong fixed flags.
5. **The way out leaks numpy's text** for a Python-built day at the int64 extreme: `#ERROR f: OverflowError: Overflow in datetime64 - datetime64 subtraction`, where every other bad date gets the seam's "is a date outside" message. Rank 3 happens to replace it.
6. **`gas_daily_fixings` accepts a (3, 1) column** and gives an odd `ValueError` for a (2, 2) array; the seam hands it 1-D columns only.
7. **`_mixed_out` accepts mismatched `kinds` and `slots` shapes that broadcast** (a (1, 3) against a (2, 3)) on a malformed record and hands the host mismatched arrays; rank 5 makes it raise.
8. **`Cells.from_rows`, a test helper, accepts a ragged range** whose later rows are shorter than the first, filling the missing cells with NaN; only a longer row raises.
