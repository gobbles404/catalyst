# _Quote

Built from [template-based-quoting.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/template-based-quoting.md), with [_CreateQuoteTemplate](quote-template.md). One whole-period indicative fixed price for a contract or strip on one market from a quote template's static terms, in the index's quote units: the fair fixed price making the equivalent trade's mark zero while every payment is still ahead, and with payments elapsed the same whole-period price under the convention below. A number, not a handle; nothing is booked, retained or reported. The code is `src/catalyst/quoting/quote.py`, the period and the quote, `src/catalyst/quoting/request.py`, since [rfq-quoting.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/rfq-quoting.md) what the quote and a request for quote share, the terms, the curve, the payments and the price of a resolved request, and `src/catalyst/excel/quoting.py`, the worksheet function. `_QuoteRFQ` prices a saved request through the same calculation, [rfq.md](rfq.md), and since [dated-quotes.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/dated-quotes.md) `_QuoteByDates` prices the days between two dates through the same path, a label's months or one window under the template's frequency, [quote-by-dates.md](quote-by-dates.md). Since [fit-to-curve-price.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/fit-to-curve-price.md) every quote says which price it asks for, `flat`, the one fixed price, or `ftc`, the fit-to-curve one, [the pricing](#the-pricing) below.

## Worksheet contract

```python
def _Quote(
    market: Handle[Market],
    contract_code: str,
    template: Handle[QuoteTemplate],
    pricing: str,
    discount_curve_set: Handle[DiscountCurveSet] | None = None,
) -> float: ...
```

The ticket's example, the market in a cell named `ngl_market`, the templates in cells named for them and the collection of named curves in `rates`:

```excel
=_Quote(ngl_market, "CAL27", ngl_otc_template, "flat", rates)
=_Quote(ngl_market, "CAL27", ngl_otc_template, "ftc", rates)
=_Quote(ngl_market, "CAL27", ngl_cleared_template, "flat")
```

The first four arguments are required. The market is the one of [_CreateMarket](../market/market.md), the history it follows supplying the index, the valuation day, the calendar and the fixings, and its vector the day's quotes. The contract code is one month or a strip in the period grammar behind [util_StartDate](../utilities/start-date.md), `Z26`, `z26`, `F27-G27`, `1H27`, `CAL27`, or since [balance-of-period-quotes.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/balance-of-period-quotes.md) the balance of one, `BALZ26`, `BALCAL27`, read from the market's valuation day as the two date utilities read it, every month of it a contract of the market's schedule; a pair of dates is [_QuoteByDates](quote-by-dates.md)'s period, never this function's. The template is a quote template and no other kind, [quote-template.md](quote-template.md); a trade template is refused by its kind, `template is trade template handle '30512:trade_template:40', expected quote template handle`. The pricing is the text `flat` or `ftc`, one of the two words exactly, typed in the formula or read from a cell, [the pricing](#the-pricing) below; it has no default, so a formula without it fails. The fifth argument is the collection of [_CollectDiscountCurves](../globals/collect-discount-curves.md), required when the template's route is bilateral and left out when it is cleared, under either pricing; a blank cell in its place is a blank, not a left-out argument, the seam's rule. The seam checks the declared types, [inputs.md](../../seam/inputs.md) and [outputs.md](../../seam/outputs.md), and the cell shows the number.

## The price

For each month `i` of the period, its floating price is its observations' weighted prices, the weights the schedule method's own, summing to one; its relative quantity is 1 for `quantity_per = month` and its calendar days, weekends and holidays included, for `calday`; and its payment factor is 1 on a cleared quote, 1 on a bilateral quote whose payment falls on or before the valuation day, and the selected curve's factor on its payment day otherwise.

```text
P_i = sum_j(weight_ij * price_ij)
q_i = 1, or the calendar days of month i
D_i = 1 cleared; 1 bilateral with payment_i <= as_of; curve(payment_i) bilateral otherwise
K   = sum_i(q_i * D_i * P_i) / sum_i(q_i * D_i)
```

The factors weight both sums, so one month with one payment quotes the same price cleared or bilateral, and a common factor on every payment cancels too; two months paying on different days do not, so a cleared price cannot be passed on to recover the bilateral one. A common quantity or unit scale cancels, and `calday` weights the months without touching the method's weights within a month: pricing days are never calendar days.

Each month's share of the price is its weight, quantity times factor, over the weights' sum, and the price is the shares' sum of the months' prices: a convex combination at each step, the observations' weights summing to one and the shares to one, so no product or sum runs past the largest number a finite input stays within, and a factor as small as the curve allows, positive by the curve's rule, scales nothing away; one month's share is one whatever its factor, which is the single-payment cancellation above. Since [rfq-quoting.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/rfq-quoting.md) each month's weight is formed from the mantissas and exponents of its quantity and its factor apart, shifted by one power of two from the largest weight's exponent, so that the largest weight is at least a quarter and none is above one: a power of two moves the exponent alone, so every price is as the plain products give it, and a factor near the smallest number, which the curve accepts, loses no month's weight to underflow. The quote refuses no arithmetic of its own: what a cell may hold is the seam's rule and a curve's factors the curve's.

Every month of the period stands in both sums whether its payment has passed, falls today or lies ahead, the agreed full-period convention: CAL27 quoted in June 2027 has January through May at their realized prices and factor 1, June at its fixed and live observations under its own payment's factor, and July through December at the day's quotes; it is never a seven-month quote, a balance of the year or a renormalization over the live observations, and a month's fixing state and its payment's timing are two facts, `payment_date <= as_of` deciding the factor and never `observation_date <= as_of`. Including paid months at factor 1 undoes no settled cash and claims nothing of a past cashflow's value; the zero-mark comparison with [_EvaluateTrades](../trades/evaluate-trades.md) holds while every payment is ahead.

## The pricing

Since [fit-to-curve-price.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/fit-to-curve-price.md) the caller says which one price is asked for, on `_Quote`, [_QuoteByDates](quote-by-dates.md) and [_QuoteRFQ](rfq.md) alike. It is a word on the formula and no term of the template or of a booking: a deal quoted either way is booked as the same regular swap.

`flat` is the price above, one fixed price for the whole period. On a curve that slopes, the early months sit on one side of one price and the late months on the other, so value moves across time, and the factors are what make it net to nothing today.

`ftc`, fit to curve, is the summary of a fixed price per month, each month fixed at its own price `P_i`: their average by quantity alone, the same sums with every factor taken as 1.

```text
flat = sum_i(q_i * D_i * P_i) / sum_i(q_i * D_i)
ftc  = sum_i(q_i * P_i)       / sum_i(q_i)
```

Each month stands at its own level, so nothing moves between months and no factor is needed; the difference between the two prices is the time value of one price standing off each month's own. Everything before the last average is the same under either word: the period, the observations, the fixings, the live prices, the nearby, the quantities and the payment days, which are made and held to their rules though `ftc` reads no factor on them, as a cleared quote's are.

The issue's example, three months at 4.50, 4.30 and 3.00 whose payments carry factors 0.99, 0.95 and 0.90:

| Weights | `flat` | `ftc` |
|---|---:|---:|
| a request's quantities, 3,100,000, 280,000 and 310,000 | `15791300 / 3614000 = 4.36948` | `16084000 / 3690000 = 4.35881` |
| a quote per `month`, one each | `11.24 / 2.84 = 3.95775` | `11.8 / 3 = 3.93333` |
| a quote per `calday`, 31, 28 and 31 days | `336.185 / 85.19 = 3.94630` | `352.9 / 90 = 3.92111` |

What follows from the two sums:

- A cleared quote already takes every factor as 1, so it is the same number under either word, and an `ftc` price on a bilateral template is the `flat` price of the same terms cleared, the same calculation and the same number to the last digit.
- One payment quotes one price under either word: one month, a `onetime` window, a one-component request, one component's share being one whatever its factor; and several components paying on one day carry one factor, which cancels. Neither is refused, since a request of several windows weighs them by quantity alone under `ftc` and by quantity and factor under `flat`, and the two differ. So they do on one row that is a strip, since a request's row is one component only where it is one month or one `onetime` window: a row of `F27-H27` under `monthly` is three components and three payments, and quotes the table's 3.95775 `flat` and 3.93333 `ftc`.
- The collection is taken the same way under both, so that one formula serves both with the one word changed and a cell can hold the word: on a bilateral template it is required, its key bound and its start checked under `ftc` as under `flat`. An `ftc` price reads no factor of it, so a curve that stops short of a late payment refuses the flat price and not the fit-to-curve one.
- The `ftc` price is a summary and not a price to book on every month. Each month booked at its own price marks zero, and so does every month at the flat price; every month at the `ftc` price marks the time value between the two, the discounted quantity times the difference, 38,569 on the example's request.

## The balance of a period

Since [balance-of-period-quotes.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/balance-of-period-quotes.md) the request may be the balance of any label the quote accepts, `BALZ26` or `BALCAL27` in either case, read exactly as [util_StartDate](../utilities/start-date.md) and [util_EndDate](../utilities/end-date.md) read it with the market's valuation day: from the later of that day and the period's first day to the period's last day. A period still ahead is the whole period, so `BALG27` asked in January is `G27`; the balance of the current period runs from the valuation day itself, which is live; a period that has ended is refused in the utilities' words. A balance is asked for by name and never inferred: `CAL27` in June keeps every month, and `BALCAL27` in June is the seven months left.

The span is split at month ends into components, the first and the last month clipped to the days the span covers, as a ticket row booked by dates is. The components then follow the rules a dated row meets: a part of a month prices under `asian` alone, its observations the pricing days within the balance, and is refused under `lastday`, `penultimate` and `last3day`; a component whose remaining days hold no pricing day is refused, and an empty first component refuses the whole strip rather than dropping it. Per month, a partial month weighs one, as the booking model weighs it; per calendar day it weighs the days the balance covers. Payments count from each component's last observation as before, so a balance pays on the day the whole month would.

On the strip of the tests, valued Monday 11 January 2027 with the 4th to the 8th fixed at 25 and F27 live at 29: `F27` quotes 28 and `BALF27` the fifteen live days alone, 29; `BALF27-G27` with G27 at 60 quotes 44.5 per month and 2289/49 per calendar day, January's balance covering 21 days against February's 28. On the calendar-year index valued 16 June: `BALCAL27` quotes June from its eleven live days alone, 42, and the six months after at 40, 282/7 per month and 7990/199 per calendar day; on a curve at 0.98 for every payment ahead the bilateral quotes equal those, every payment carrying one factor, where `CAL27`'s paid months at factor one keep it at 424.4/11.86. An equivalent trade booked on the balance's own dates at the quote marks zero on either route under either basis.

## Work

The quote reports what it did, in the counters the pipeline's stages use for the same facts: the template's values read, the schedule's codes read, the months resolved, the observations made, the fixings read, one per fixed observation, the live prices read, one per distinct live contract, the payment rule read and its instructions applied once over the months, the payment days made, the curve selected and, under `flat`, the payment factors read, one per distinct payment day after the valuation day, none read and none counted under `ftc`, and four reductions whatever the strip, the months' prices, the largest weight's exponent, the weights and the price. Each request is resolved whole on every call; nothing is cached between calls, and an unchanged call recomputes the same number.

## Not here
