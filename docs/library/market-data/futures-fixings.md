# _CreateFuturesFixings

Built in #75; checked against the market's holiday calendar since #79; the futures' name and two columns since #101; the index since #108; taken newest first since #128.

The futures fixings of one index: the settlement price on each day it fixed, date beside settle, as the desk keeps them, the newest at the top, one for every business day of its market from the last row's day to the first row's, kept oldest first and reached by handle. It is the futures market's own fixings, as the [IFERC fixings](iferc-fixings.md) are the IFERC market's and the [Gas Daily fixings](gas-daily-fixings.md) the Gas Daily market's, and a history that instruments will settle against, as opposed to the prices that will price them. Since #79 the creator checks the days against the market's holiday calendar, the first creator to take a handle in, `util_ReadHandle` having taken any handle since #66, and refuses a fixing on a weekend day or a holiday and a business day without one, so a series pasted short, or with a closed day in it, fails at its creator rather than at the swap that settles on it. From #108 the holiday calendar comes through the [index](../index-definitions/index.md), which since #107 holds it beside the schedule: the creator takes the index, reads the holiday calendar off it, keeps the index by reference so that the fixings say whose they are, and refuses an index of another kind, an IFERC or a Gas Daily index having no holiday calendar and no settles. Since #84 [_CreateMarketHistory](../market/market-history.md) reads them, binding the settle on each past business day of the index's calendar to it, and takes them as `futures_fixings` from #101 and as `fixings` from #105, one argument for the fixings of any market. Built in #75 as `_CreateDailyFixings` from a header row and a table, the creator is `_CreateFuturesFixings` from #101 and takes two columns, the date and the settle, selected without the header row, as its siblings do: the desk found the header row and the body clunky in use, one form and one naming across the fixings family, the market's name then `Fixings`, is worth more than the table's guard against a misaligned selection, and a column of numbers costs the seam no look and no temporary per cell. From #128 the series is given as the desk keeps it, the newest fixing in the first row, so that a new day is a row added at the top and the row the desk reads first; the creator checks the rows as the sheet gave them, naming the sheet's own row when one is wrong, and then keeps both columns the other way up, oldest first, the order the [market history](../market/market-history.md) reads, each settle beside its own day, so the history and the market are unchanged. One direction is taken and nothing is sorted: a series pasted oldest first, as it was given before #128, fails at its second row, and a series of one row is a series either way. The IFERC fixings keep their freedom of order, having no date to read a direction from. The record, `FuturesFixings`, keeps its two arrays and, from #108, its index; its readers do not change, and its spill runs newest first, as the creator takes it, so the spill's two columns handed back to the creator make the same fixings. The code is `src/catalyst/market_data/futures_fixings.py`, the record and its builder, and `src/catalyst/excel/market_data.py`, the worksheet function.

**More than one nearby.** A nearby is a contract counted from the prompt, the listed contract nearest its expiry on a day: the first nearby is the prompt itself and the second the next contract the schedule lists. `settle` is a range of numbers, a row per date and a column per nearby, the prompt's first: one column, `B2:B5` above, is the series every sheet has had, the prompt contract's settle on each day, and a range two columns wide, `F2:G5` in row 8, carries the second nearby's settle beside it, for a swap that floats on that contract, [nearby-selection.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/nearby-selection.md). The fixings hold positions and no contract: which contract a column's settle is of on a day is the calendar's to say, so the [market history](../market/market-history.md) holds each to a listed contract and shows its code beside the settle. Every cell holds a number, a blank or a text among the settles being refused by its cell, so no day is without a nearby's settle and none is ever carried forward or taken from another column. The second column must roll as the calendar does, the expiry day belonging to the expiring contract: a vendor's series that rolls on another day cannot be told apart here, a price being a number, and the history's readback is where to check it.

## The library checks

Five rules, in this order, the first failure being the message. The first is the index's kind: the days are checked against a holiday calendar, and only a futures index has one, so an IFERC or a Gas Daily index is refused naming its kind, as the calendar names kinds. The second comes with the ranges: the settles have one row per date, whatever their width, since the seam never compares two ranges and a series is rows. The third is the order, newest first since #128, which the owner stated as no duplicate dates and the dates descending: each date is before the row above, and that one comparison covers both, keeping one fixing per day and the newest at the top. The fourth and fifth are the ticket's of #79, that creation detects a fixing on a day the market was closed and a business day without a fixing: each date is a business day, Monday to Friday and not a holiday, at either end of the series as in the middle; and no business day between the last row's date and the first row's is without a fixing. With every date a business day and the dates decreasing, the series is whole exactly when each date is the business day before the one above, which is exactly when the business days from the last row's date up to the first row's number one fewer than the rows: one `np.busday_count` of two dates, and only when that count is short one `np.busday_offset` over the column and one comparison to name the first business day without a fixing, as [time.md](../time.md) decided business days would be counted: numpy's functions over the calendar's list, a week of Monday to Friday, and no loop. The rules run on the rows as the sheet gave them, so each message names the sheet's own row, the first row of the column being row 1, and within a rule the first row from the top that breaks it; a rule earlier in the order is reported before a later one, whichever row each is on, so a date out of order low in the column is reported before a holiday in its first row. The message names the column by its argument name.

| Rule | Message |
|---|---|
| the index is a futures index, whose holiday calendar the days are checked against | `index is a Gas Daily index, expected a futures index` |
| the settles have one row per date, a column per nearby | `settle has 3 rows, expected 4, one per date` |
| each date before the row above | `date row 2 is 2026-05-05, expected a date before row 1's 2026-05-04` |
| each date a business day, Monday to Friday and not a holiday | `date row 1 is 2026-05-02, a Saturday, expected a business day`, or `date row 2 is 2026-05-25, a holiday, expected a business day` |
| every business day from the last row's date to the first row's has a fixing | `date row 2 is 2026-05-04, expected 2026-05-05, the business day before row 1's 2026-05-06` |

A count of one reads `1 row`, as a row of settles beside several dates does. The two end dates bound the check: nothing is asked for after the first row's date or before the last row's, so a series from a Thursday back to the Tuesday of the same week is whole, and a holiday outside the period changes nothing, so a calendar of one day in January checks a series in May against Monday to Friday alone. A single business day is a series; a single Saturday or holiday is refused. The calendar is read as numpy reads it, a day listed twice counting once and a day listed on a weekend adding nothing, and a Saturday in the series is a Saturday whether or not the calendar lists it. Nothing is sorted: a series pasted oldest first fails at its second row, as a date given twice does, and the desk turns it over, because nothing in the library sorts anything and one direction is the only one taken; nothing is filled in either, since a fixing the desk did not paste is a row to paste, never a price to invent. Nothing else is checked: whether the days are the index's schedule's, or the settles its contracts', is nothing the fixings can say, and the index is kept for what it is, the one the desk paired the series with. A settle is any number a cell holds, zero and below included, WTI's May 2020 contract having settled below zero, and a cell holds no NaN or infinity to refuse. What a settle may be for a given index is a reader's rule, written when the reader is. One fixing is a series. Both columns are copied from the last row to the first and frozen, one copy each as before, so the fixings own their data and keep it oldest first, each settle beside its own day, the days as days; the inputs are left as they were, and the index is kept by reference. A failed call publishes nothing, and a column the seam rejects never reaches the function.
