# _CreateIndex

Built in #107; the specifications' lot units checked against the conventions since #115; the reporting lot, the specification a batch's delta is reported in lots of, named and resolved since #164, [lot-delta-reporting.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/lot-delta-reporting.md); the display name, a description beside the name, since #169, [readable-index-names.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/readable-index-names.md).

The index behind one reference price: its name, its schedule, the conventions its price is quoted in, the contract specifications it trades as and, for a futures index, the holiday calendar of its market, chosen by key from a collection, [holiday-keys.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/holiday-keys.md); objects the sheet already holds and a name, bound once and reached by handle. Henry Hub NYMEX, Henry Hub IFERC and Henry Hub Gas Daily are three indices, each with a schedule of its own kind, and the kind of the schedule is the kind of the index. Each object was checked when it was made, and since #115 the index reads one fact out of two of them: every lot unit of the specifications is a unit of the conventions' quantity type, a rule neither could hold alone. Nothing else in them is checked again; the index binds them so that what is downstream can refer to one thing. Since #164 the index may also name one of the specifications as its reporting lot, the one every batch of trades on it counts its delta in, resolved here once into the lot's quantity in the quote quantity unit and the settlement currency a live lot makes per unit of quote price, the number the kernel's preparation divides by and the risk report names; an index made without one builds markets as before and refuses a batch of trades when it is made. Its readers today: the [calendar](../calendar/calendar.md), since #108, reads its kind, its schedule and its holiday calendar in place of taking the two itself; the three fixings, the [futures](../market-data/futures-fixings.md), the [Gas Daily](../market-data/gas-daily-fixings.md) and the [published](../market-data/published-fixings.md) fixings, each refuse an index of another kind and keep it, the futures fixings reading its holiday calendar and the published fixings the dates of its schedule; the [market history](../market/market-history.md), since #109, requires its calendar's index and its fixings' to be one object; and the [market](../market/market.md), which counts its quotes against the index's schedule and, since #112, compares its price vector's name with the index's. Past the index's own rule below and the spills of [util_ReadHandle](../utilities/read-handle.md), nothing reads the conventions or the specifications yet: the instruments to come will read units, lots and fees through the market they price against, as the market reads its calendar. The code is `src/catalyst/index_definitions/index.py`, the record and its builder, `src/catalyst/index_definitions/kind.py`, the kinds and the holiday rules the index shares with the calendar and the fixings since #107, and `src/catalyst/excel/index_definitions.py`, the worksheet function.


Two texts, three handles, a key, text and the collection's handle, the last three of which may be left out. The name is text as typed, one cell, never trimmed or re-cased, and a label: two indices may share a name, and nothing looks an index up by it; since #113 it shows in the handle of the index and of everything made over it, `30512:index:Henry Hub NYMEX:10`, as [handles.md](../../seam/handles.md#the-handles-text) says. The display name, since #169, is text as typed too, one cell, a description a reader who does not know the desk's short names would understand, `Henry Hub natural gas - NYMEX`: required, never defaulted to the name and free to repeat it; nothing reads it but the readback, and the handle, the price vector's match and every message keep the name, [readable-index-names.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/readable-index-names.md). The schedule is any handle, the form [inputs.md](../../seam/inputs.md) decision 40 gave `util_ReadHandle` and the calendar took in #104: the seam resolves it and hands over whatever object it finds, and the library says whether it is a schedule and of which kind. The conventions and the specifications are handles of one kind each, and a cell of another kind fails in the seam's words, `index_conventions is holiday calendar handle '30512:holiday_calendar:NYMEX:6', expected index conventions handle`, before the function runs; the specifications are required, since every index trades as at least one contract. The holiday key is text, one calendar's name or several joined by `+`, `NYMEX+ICE` the calendar closed when either is, [collect-holiday-calendars.md](collect-holiday-calendars.md#binding-a-key): it takes decision 39's form, left out with nothing in its place for a Gas Daily or a published index, required for a futures one, and a blank cell in its place is a blank, refused by the seam. The collection the key selects from is the last argument, a handle of its own kind: required with a key, and read for nothing without one, so a published index may name it or leave it out. The index holds the calendar the key selected and nothing later looks a name up. Empty text, a blank, a number and text naming no live object fail in the seam's words, each argument alone, left to right. The result is a handle: text in the cell that stands for the index the seam keeps, declared `Handle[Index]`, as [bridge.md](../../seam/bridge.md#a-handle-one-store-and-the-text) says. A successful call publishes an index; a `_CreateIndex` call with the same arguments as a live one, in another cell or on a recalculation, shares that index and its handle rather than making another, and the index is released when Excel disconnects the last creator call that shares it, as [handles.md](../../seam/handles.md#publication-and-release) says. The reporting lot, since #164, is text naming one of the specifications' names exactly, no trimming, no case folding and no alias, decision 39's form as the holiday key takes: left out, the index has none, as an index that only builds markets needs none; a blank cell in its place is a blank, refused by the seam; a name the specifications lack is refused by the library naming them.

`=util_ReadHandle(A8)` shows what the index holds, one row under the two heading rows: the name, since #169 the display name beside it, the kind, how many contracts the schedule has, how many contracts the specifications name, whether it has a holiday calendar and, since #164, the reporting lot, `unset` on an index made without one. The parts themselves read back through their own handles, and reading an index publishes nothing.

| name | display_name | kind | contracts | specifications | has_holiday_calendar | reporting_lot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| str | str | str | int64 | int64 | bool | str |
| Henry Hub NYMEX | Henry Hub natural gas - NYMEX | futures | 12 | 2 | TRUE | unset |

`=util_ReadHandle(A9)` on the monthly published index shows `Henry Hub IFERC`, `Henry Hub natural gas - Inside FERC monthly index`, `published`, `3`, `1`, `FALSE`, `unset`, and the Gas Daily one `Gas Daily` in the third column. `=util_ReadHandle(A11)` on the index naming its lot shows `lots` in the seventh column and four columns more, `lot_size`, `lot_unit`, `lot_quote_quantity` and `lot_coefficient`: `10000`, `MMBTU`, `10000` and `10000`, the lot in the quote quantity unit and the dollars a live lot makes per dollar an MMBTU, the two conversions resolved once; on an index quoted in cents a gallon and settled in dollars a lot of a thousand barrels reads `42000` and `420`. The kind is spelled as every message spells it, `futures`, `Gas Daily` and `published`.

## The library checks

Five rules, in this order, the first failure being the message. The first is the calendar's rule of #104 in the calendar's words, the calendar having taken the index since #108; the second is the holiday key's, the kind read before the key is looked up; the third, since #115, is the one fact that lives across two of the definitions; the last two, since #164, are the reporting lot's and run only when one is named.

| Rule | Message |
|---|---|
| the schedule is a futures, a Gas Daily or a [published](published-schedule.md) schedule, its kind read off the object as the calendar reads it, and an object of another kind is refused naming what it is | `schedule is a holiday calendar, expected a futures, Gas Daily or published schedule` |
| a futures index names its holiday calendar by key, since its market closes on days its schedule does not say, and the key selects from the collection by the rule every key has; a Gas Daily or a published index leaves the key out, since nothing of theirs closes a day, and one given is refused before it is looked up | `holiday_key is missing, expected a holiday calendar's key with a futures schedule`; `holiday_key is 'NYMEX', expected none with a Gas Daily schedule`; `holiday_key is 'NYMEX', expected none with a published schedule`; `holiday_calendars is missing, expected a _CollectHolidayCalendars collection, which holiday_key selects a calendar from`; `holiday_key is 'NYMX', expected names of the collection's calendars, 'NYMEX' and 'ICE'; it holds no 'NYMX'` |
| every lot unit of the specifications is a unit of the conventions' quantity type, because a trade's count of a word converts to the index's quantity through that unit, so barrels and gallons both stand under a volume index and an energy unit does not; the row, the unit and the units that would have done are named, in the tables' order | `specifications row 2 lot_unit is 'MMBTU', expected a VOLUME unit, GAL, BBL, L or M3` |
| the reporting lot, where named, is one of the specifications' names, matched exactly, since the delta of every batch on the index is a count of that row and a guess would count the wrong thing; the names that would have done are listed in the specifications' order | `reporting_lot is 'LOT', expected one of the specifications: 'lots', 'ice_lots', 'mmbtu'` |
| the lot restated in the quote quantity unit and in the settlement currency is finite, since a lot size that outruns the largest number would divide every delta into nothing | `reporting_lot 'LOT' is inf GAL a lot, expected a finite quantity; the lot size outruns the largest number` |

Nothing else is checked. The name is a label, kept as typed, neither unique nor looked up, and the display name, since #169, a description kept as typed, read by the readback alone. Whether the schedule, the conventions and the specifications are one index's is otherwise the desk's to keep right, since nothing in any of them says which index it belongs to; the specifications' names are the desk's words for a quantity of the index, `lots`, `ice_lots` and `mmbtu`, and neither their names nor their count answer to the schedule's codes or its count. What each part must hold alone was checked by its own creator and is not checked again: the conventions' codes since #110, the specifications' names, lot sizes, lot units and fees since #78, #110 and #115, each on its own page. The calendar's rules on a schedule, its order and its count of contracts, stay the [calendar](../calendar/calendar.md#the-library-checks)'s, so an index that builds says its parts are of the right kinds and its lots of its measure, and nothing more. The objects are kept by reference and unchanged, the calendar the key selected among them, and the record holds the kind; nothing is copied and nothing built but the small record, and the union a key of several names makes, and the one pass is the third rule's, one lookup per row of the specifications in the conventions' measure, never a look at the schedule. A failed call publishes nothing.

## Not here

The calendar and the futures fixings take the index since #108, `_CreateCalendar(valuation_date, index)` and `_CreateFuturesFixings(date, settle, index)`, the calendar reading the kind the index holds and both reading the holiday calendar through it; the two rules above live here alone. The key's form and its binding are [collect-holiday-calendars.md](collect-holiday-calendars.md#binding-a-key)'s. The Gas Daily and the published fixings take theirs too, and the [market history](../market/market-history.md) requires the calendar's index and the fixings' to be one object since #109, so that every reader of a calendar and its fixings reads one index. The conventions' codes are checked against the unit tables since #110, and the specifications' lot sizes and fees hold their rules since #110, each on its own page, [conventions](index-conventions.md) and [specifications](contract-specifications.md); the price scale the codes imply is the instrument's to look up, and the fees are costs per lot in the conventions' settlement currency, read off the index by the consumer that charges them. Whether the parts are one index's: the desk's, since a name is a label; there is no registry of indices, no lookup by name and no uniqueness. The discount curve: the valuation's, never the index's. Pricing and risk, but for the lot the delta is reported in, named and resolved here since #164 and read by the kernel's preparation. A reader of its own: [util_ReadHandle](../utilities/read-handle.md) spills the summary above, and each part spills as its own page shows.
