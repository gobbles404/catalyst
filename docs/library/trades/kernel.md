# The trade kernel

Built in #136 as ticket I1 of the [trade-valuation pipeline](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/README.md). Since #139, ticket I4, [_CreateTrades](create-trades.md) resolves the terms from a template and a ticket on the sheet and publishes the batch, the terms and the encoding together; the record gained the instrument, the clearing house and broker labels and the payment terms, and a lot count or a price is refused naming the trade's own row, which a strip's component row is not. Since #140, ticket I4a, the kernel's axis is the dated slot, [dated-exposure.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/dated-exposure.md): an observation day within a payment group, with the contract pricing that day as its reference, so that an observation fixing on its own day is set while the rest of the batch stays live; a `lastday` future has one slot per contract, its expiry day, and every number of the NG example stands. Since #141, ticket I5, [_PrepareTrades](prepare-trades.md), [_EvaluateTrades](evaluate-trades.md), [_ReportValues](report-values.md) and [_ReportRisk](report-risk.md) stand on it, the records `PreparedTrades` and `TradeResults` named for the handle words they publish under. Since #146, [zero-quantities.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/zero-quantities.md), a quantity of zero is a whole count like any other, a row kept ready for a deal, carried through every step and contributing nothing, its contract on the axis at a delta of zero.

The four steps of the pipeline as library code, `src/goosedove/trades/`: the resolved terms of a batch and their observations, their static encoding on dated slots, the batch prepared against one market history, and its evaluation against one market. One occurrence takes the same path as a thousand and every occurrence counts, a duplicate twice. The [NG example](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/ng-creation-preparation-example.md) is the acceptance fixture: every hand answer on that page, the real NG rows before expiry and the toy lifecycle through its seven states, is pinned in `tests/unit/test_trades.py`, with the daily toy and the offsetting dates of the dated-exposure page, and the work of each step in `tests/performance/test_trade_budgets.py`.

## The five records

| Record | Builder | What it holds | What makes a new one |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ResolvedTrades` | `resolved_trades` | the instrument, the index and its futures schedule, the specification row, the one selection the sheet's quantity unit named, the route, the settlement, the schedule method and the basis; per occurrence its trade date and, where given, its label, clearing house and broker; per component its occurrence, its schedule row, its signed quantity, a count of the specification, and its fixed price; for a financial settlement the payment terms, `PaymentTerms`, per occurrence the lag in business days and the calendar it is counted on and per component the day they give | a change to the ticket, the template or a definition used |
| `Observations` | `observations`; `observed` gives a batch's by its schedule method, the index and the components' schedule rows | per observation the component it prices, the day whose settle it takes and its weight, the share of the component's quantity it carries, a component's weights summing to one; `lastday` gives each component one, its contract's expiry day, and `penultimate` one, the business day before it on the index's holiday calendar, each with the whole weight | the same |
| `Encoded` | `encoded` | per component the signed quote quantity, the count times the specification's size in the quote quantity unit, the coefficient, the settlement currency it makes per unit of quote price, and the entry-price offset in the settlement currency; per payment group, one per contract held in the schedule's order, the summed offset and the one payment day; per slot, an observation day within a group, in group order and by day, the day, the reference, the schedule row of the contract pricing that day, derived by the calendar's rule and retained, and the exposure, the components' coefficients times their observations' weights summed, which every settle or quote of the day multiplies; each observation's slot; the delta axis, the distinct references, and each slot's place on it | the same |
| `PreparedTrades` | `prepared_trades` | per slot whether it has fixed and its live exposure, zero where fixed; per group its set amount, the fixed slots' exposure times their settles, and whether it is settled; the live slots, the distinct references they quote off and each live slot's place among them; the unsettled offsets and set amounts summed, and the cash; delta by reference and its total, which no tick changes | a new history: a fixing arrived or corrected, or the valuation date rolled |
| `TradeResults` | `trade_results` | the route, the currency and the valuation day; mark and cash; per live slot its dated contribution, exposure times its reference's quote; the batch's reference axis, the preparation's delta and total shared, and DV01, with their unit | a new market: a tick, or anything that made a new preparation |

Each record keeps the one before it by reference, so a result explains itself back to the terms and the index, and every array is owned and read-only. Nothing in the kernel imports the Excel bridge; the unit tables and the work counter arrive as arguments and are never worksheet parameters. The fixing source is one for the batch, the index and the history over it, kept once and repeated in no slot.

## What each step does, and refuses

**The terms.** A futures index, since a future's contract is a row of a futures schedule and its final price the settlement on that row's expiry day; a row of its specifications, since the lot size and the lot unit are read there, the one selection, whatever the desk's word for it, an exchange lot or a bare unit the index defines with a size of one. One or more occurrences, a date each and, for each label tuple given, a text each; one or more components, each of an occurrence, every occurrence holding at least one, each contract a row of the schedule, each quantity a whole number, zero included, since a row kept ready for a deal holds a zero and contributes nothing, each fixed price finite, the quantity and the price named by the trade they belong to, as the sheet counts its rows. A financial settlement has payment terms, a lag and a calendar per occurrence and one payment day per component; a physical one has none, since it pays on no day and terms given would invent a payment. A penultimate future settles financially, `settlement is 'physical', expected 'financial' with the penultimate method; a penultimate future is cash settled on the business day before its contract's expiry`. The instrument is kept as the definition the terms stand under and read for nothing here. The quantity and the price rules, `whole_quantities` and `finite_prices`, are callable on their own, and the template reads its numbers through them.

| Rule broken | Message |
|---|---|
| the index is not a futures index | `index is a Gas Daily index, expected a futures index for a future` |
| the specification is no row of the index's | `specification is 1, expected a row of the index's specifications, 0 to 0` |
| an occurrence holds no component | `trade 'NG-001' at row 5 has no component, expected at least one` |
| a contract is no row of the schedule | `contract row 4 is 3, expected a row of the schedule, 0 to 2` |
| a quantity is not a whole number, a fraction, a NaN or an infinity | `quantity of trade 'NG-003' at row 3 is 7.5, expected a whole number`; `quantity of trade 'NG-003' at row 3 is nan, expected a whole number` |
| a fixed price is not finite | `fixed_price of trade 'NG-004' at row 4 is inf, expected a finite price` |
| a financial settlement without its terms, with a lag or a calendar not per occurrence or days not per component, or a physical one with terms | `payment is missing, expected a lag and a calendar per occurrence and one date per component with a financial settlement`; `payment lag_days has 4, expected 5, one per occurrence`; `payment date has 1 dates, expected 5, one per component`; `payment has 5 dates, expected none with a physical settlement, which pays on no day` |

**The observations.** Aligned, in component order with each component's days increasing, every component holding at least one, every weight positive and each component's weights summing to one within rounding, a seventh seven times included. `observed` gives a batch's by its schedule method, the index and the components' schedule rows: `lastday`, one per component on its contract's expiry day with the whole weight; `penultimate`, since #144, one per component on the business day before that expiry, Monday to Friday less the index's holiday calendar, one `numpy.busday_offset` over the components, an expiry the market is closed on refused by its contract, `Z26 expires on 2026-11-26, a day the index's market is closed; the penultimate method takes the business day before an expiry the market prices`, since the day before a closed expiry is not the last day the market priced the contract and rolling the expiry would change the schedule the desk gave. The resolution makes the observations before the payment terms and counts each payment from its component's observation day, so a payment day is never counted from a day nothing observes. The tests give the daily toy's through the same builder, three a component with a third each, no public method naming them.

| Rule broken | Message |
|---|---|
| a column of another length | `date has 1, expected 2, one per observation` |
| no observation at all | `component has 0 observations, expected at least one per component` |
| a component outside the batch, or out of order | `observation 2 is of component 2, expected a component, 0 to 1`; `observation 2 is of component 0, after one of component 1; expected component order` |
| a component's days not increasing | `observation 2 of component 0 is on 2026-12-02, expected a day after 2026-12-02, the observation before it` |
| a component without an observation | `component 1 has no observation, expected at least one` |
| a weight not positive, or weights not summing to one | `weight 2 is 0.0, expected a positive share`; `the weights of component 0 sum to 1.1, expected 1, the whole quantity shared out` |

**The encoding.** The quote quantity is the quantity, a count of the selected specification, times that specification's lot size, restated in the conventions' quote quantity unit through the unit tables, both units of the quantity type as the index checked. The coefficient is the quote quantity restated from the quote currency to the settlement currency, so that a price in cents times it is an amount in dollars; every price the batch meets, a quote, a settle or the fixed price, multiplies the coefficient and never the quote quantity, which is kept apart as the physical fact. The offset is minus the coefficient times the fixed price. The groups are the distinct contracts held, sorted, which is the schedule's order, the offsets summed by group, so two occurrences on one contract are one group with their offsets added, a duplicate twice. Each observation's day is one the index's market is open on, Monday to Friday less the index's holidays, by the calendar's rule, `open_days`, else refused: no settle exists for a closed day or ever will, and a live observation on one would take a quote today and fail the day it fixed, which the review of #140 caught. Each observation's reference is the contract pricing its day by the calendar's rule, `contract_on`, the first whose expiry is on or after it, one search over the schedule; it is the component's own contract, else the observation is refused as unsupported, since a futures history holds one settle a day, the pricing contract's, and would never fix another contract's observation on it: F27's on a day Z26 prices. The slots are the distinct days within each group, one lexsort, each observation's coefficient times its weight summed into its slot, one bincount, so two occurrences observing one day in one group are one slot with their exposures added. The delta axis is the distinct references. For a financial settlement every component in a group pays on that group's one day, and that day is after the group's last observation. The fees of the specification are read by nothing: reserves, never a contribution to value.

| Rule broken | Message |
|---|---|
| an observation on a day the market is closed, a weekend or a holiday, whether it has passed or not | `trade 'NG-001' at row 1 observes Z26 on 2026-11-26, a day the index's market is closed; no settle exists or will` |
| an observation after the schedule's last expiry | `trade 'G-1' at row 1 observes 2027-02-01, after the schedule's last expiry, 2027-01-29; nothing prices it` |
| an observation of a contract other than the day's pricing contract | `trade 'G-1' at row 1 observes G27 on 2026-12-01, a day Z26 prices; an observation of a contract other than the day's pricing contract has no settle in a futures history, not supported` |
| two payment days in one group, the group's first component and the first paying on another day both named with their days, neither held to be the right one, since #150 | `Z26 pays on 2026-11-30 for trade 'NG-001' at row 1 and on 2026-12-01 for trade 'NG-002' at row 2, expected one payment day per contract` |
| a payment on or before the group's last observation | `F27 pays on 2026-12-29, expected a day after its last observation, 2026-12-29, when its final price exists` |

For the NG rows, quote quantity `[100000, -40000, 70000, -120000, 100000]` MMBTU, the coefficient the same since dollars are quoted and paid, offset `[-310000, 126000, -238000, 402000, -310000]` USD, two groups, Z26 and F27 at rows 1 and 2 with offsets `[-494000, 164000]`, and two slots, Z26 on 25 November and F27 on 29 December, each referencing itself, with exposures `[160000, -50000]`, as the page says. One lot bought at 300 cents on an index quoted in cents and settled in dollars has a quantity of 10,000 MMBTU, a coefficient of 100 USD per cent per MMBTU and an offset of -30,000 USD: at 300 cents it marks zero, a cent moves it 100 dollars, and ten cents up it marks 1,000, live, fixed or paid.

**The preparation.** The history is over the trades' index, the one object. A slot has fixed when the calendar marks its day fixed, the day after it at the earliest: an observation stays live on its own day, as the calendar's rule says. A physical future whose contract has expired, its expiry day marked fixed, is refused, naming the trade, the contract, its expiry and the valuation date, since delivery is not modeled and nothing is omitted, zeroed, kept in mark or moved to cash in its place; the contract's expiry decides, not an earlier observation's fixing. A fixed slot takes its settle off the history, the settle on its day, one read per fixed slot, which the history holds for every fixed and priced day, every slot's day being priced since the encoding refused a closed one against the same holiday calendar; a group is settled when every slot of it has fixed and its payment day is on or before the valuation date, the pipeline's inclusive cutoff, and fixed is not paid. The live slots are the rest, with the distinct references they quote off and each slot's place among them for the tick. Delta is settled here too, the live exposure summed by reference over the whole axis, zero where everything has fixed, with its total, since nothing a tick brings changes either.

| Rule broken | Message |
|---|---|
| the history is another index's | `history is over another index, 'Henry Hub NYMEX', expected the trades', 'Henry Hub NYMEX'` |
| a slot on or before the schedule's first expiry, which bounds the calendar | `trade at row 1 observes X26 on 2026-10-28, before the calendar begins; the schedule's first contract bounds the calendar and nothing prices it` |
| a physical contract past its expiry | `trade 'NG-001' at row 1 on Z26 expired 2026-11-25, before valuation date 2026-11-26; a physical future after its expiry is not supported` |

**The evaluation.** The market is over the history the batch was prepared against, the one object; a market over another history is refused, and the batch is prepared again outside, never silently here. The route is cleared; a bilateral batch is refused by name until discount factors and a DV01 convention exist, rather than valued as if undiscounted. The market answers one quote per live reference and refuses a contract past its coverage by name, as it does for every reader; the quotes are gathered onto the live slots, each slot's exposure times its reference's quote is its dated contribution, and the live amount is their sum; the mark is the unsettled offsets and set amounts plus the live amount; the cash is the preparation's. Delta and its total are the preparation's, shared and never written, over the batch's whole axis, the references in the schedule's order whatever has fixed, the total the change for a common move of one in every contract; DV01 is zero, the undiscounted cleared model's answer and not a placeholder. The dated contributions are retained with the result and allocate neither the offsets nor the mark among dates.

| Rule broken | Message |
|---|---|
| the market is over another history | `market is over another history of index 'Henry Hub NYMEX', expected the one the trades were prepared against` |
| the route is bilateral | `route is bilateral, expected cleared; bilateral valuation needs discount factors and a DV01 convention, not built yet` |
| a quote is missing for a referenced contract | the market's own words, `requested contract 'F27' is beyond live-price coverage ending at 'Z26'` |

For the NG rows valued on 24 November 2026 at quotes `[3.20, 3.50]`: dated contributions `[512000, -175000]`, mark 7,000 USD, cash 0, delta `[160000, -50000]` USD per USD/MMBTU, total 110,000; at `[3.21, 3.50]` the mark is 8,600 and the delta unchanged. The five singletons mark `[10000, -2000, 7000, -18000, 10000]` and sum to the batch; the first four alone mark -3,000.

## Work

Each builder reports to its counter what it did, in counts: the encoding one component per component, one observation per observation and one slot per slot; the preparation one slot per slot and one settle read per fixed slot, nothing per component, nothing per observation and nothing per day of the calendar; the evaluation one kernel call and one quote read per live reference. The daily batch of the budgets shows the axis sized by slots: two thousand trades over twenty-four contracts and fifteen days are thirty thousand observations into 360 slots, the preparation allocating about 86 bytes a slot and the tick about 22, nothing per trade or per observation, and the result retaining eight bytes per live slot. A tick, a new market over the same preparation, runs the evaluation alone: `trade_results` once, `Market.live_prices` once, and the counter, the same functions at five components and at two thousand, with nothing of the encoding or the preparation done again; the gather and the multiplication over the live slots are dispatched in C, as the dot product was. The numpy calls cProfile sees on a tick are the market's one look at the references asked for with its reduction, the sum of the contributions and their freezing, as [measuring.md](../../seam/measuring.md) explains, and delta is not made again.

## Not here

The template, the ticket, the field names, the contract codes read for their month and the payment days counted from a lag and a calendar are [create-trades.md](create-trades.md)'s, which hands the kernel resolved terms; the toy's days still arrive resolved in these tests, and the daily toy's observations through the builder. No bilateral valuation, discount factor or DV01 convention, no `asian` or `last3day` as a public method, no payment convention for a method not yet built, no `day` quantity basis, no quantity unit the index does not define, no cash instrument, no market whose whole calendar has ended, and no observation of a contract other than the day's pricing contract, which waits for a history that holds a settle per contract per day.
