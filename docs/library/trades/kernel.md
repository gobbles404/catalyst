# The trade kernel

Built in #136 as ticket I1 of the [trade-valuation pipeline](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/README.md). Since #139, ticket I4, [_CreateTrades](create-trades.md) resolves the terms from a template and a ticket on the sheet and publishes the batch, the terms and the encoding together; the record gained the instrument, the clearing house and broker labels and the payment terms, and a quantity or a price is refused naming the trade's own row, which a strip's component row is not. Since #140, ticket I4a, the kernel's axis is the dated slot, [dated-exposure.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/dated-exposure.md): an observation day within a payment group, with the contract pricing that day as its reference, so that an observation fixing on its own day is set while the rest of the batch stays live; a `lastday` future has one slot per contract, its expiry day, and every number of the NG example stands. Since #141, ticket I5, [_PrepareTrades](prepare-trades.md), [_EvaluateTrades](evaluate-trades.md), [_ReportValues](report-values.md) and [_ReportRisk](report-risk.md) stand on it, the records `PreparedTrades` and `TradeResults` named for the handle words they publish under. Since #146, [zero-quantities.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/zero-quantities.md), a quantity of zero is a quantity like any other, a row kept ready for a deal, carried through every step and contributing nothing, its contract on the axis at a delta of zero. Since [fractional-quantities.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/fractional-quantities.md), a quantity's rule follows the batch's settlement: any finite number under a financial settlement, a lot and a half being 1.5, used as entered and never rounded, and a whole number under a physical one, whose position is closed by whole counts adding to exactly zero. Since #162, [multiple-payment-groups.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/multiple-payment-groups.md), a payment group is a contract and the day it pays: a contract whose components pay on two days is two groups, each moving from mark to cash on its own day, while the delta axis holds the contract once and a tick reads its quote once. Since #163, [mixed-trade-fields.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/mixed-trade-fields.md), each occurrence has its own specification, schedule method and quantity basis, so one batch mixes units, methods and bases row by row and values through the one path.

The four steps of the pipeline as library code, `src/catalyst/trades/`: the resolved terms of a batch and their observations, their static encoding on dated slots, the batch prepared against one market history per leg, and its evaluation against one market per leg, one leg or since [two-index-fixed-float.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/two-index-fixed-float.md)'s second pull request a pair. One occurrence takes the same path as a thousand and every occurrence counts, a duplicate twice. The [NG example](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/ng-creation-preparation-example.md) is the acceptance fixture: every hand answer of mark, cash and delta on that page, the real NG rows before expiry and the toy lifecycle through its seven states, is pinned in `tests/unit/test_trades.py`, the page's per-group amounts at historical prices pinned only as each state's set total, with the daily toy and the offsetting dates of the dated-exposure page, and the work of each step in `tests/performance/test_trade_budgets.py`.

The four schedule methods, their names and encoded positions, are `trades/schedule_methods.py`'s, with the facts the stages share about them, which a published index takes, which permit physical settlement, which take the whole month, which count back from the expiry and which price off the booked contract, named there as rules; the terms, the observations and the encoding apply them at their own stages, and each check and its refusal stays where it is.

## What each step does, and refuses

**The terms.** A futures index or a [published](../index-definitions/published-schedule.md) index, since a fixed_float's contract is a row of a futures schedule, its final price the settlement on that row's expiry day, or a month of a published schedule, its final price the average of the month's prints or, where the month has one print, that print, the traded basis on a monthly index such as Inside FERC's, [iferc-basis-futures.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/iferc-basis-futures.md); a Gas Daily index is refused by name. A published index takes a financial settlement, nothing being delivered against a published price, and two methods. `asian` is the average of the prints the period covers, an equal share each. `lastday` is the one print of a month that has one, the method a trade on a monthly index is booked under: the row covers its whole month and the month has exactly one print, which the observations below hold it to. On a month of one print `asian` is that print, the price `lastday` gives. `penultimate` and `last3day` count business days back from an expiry, and a published index has none, so the first row of either is named, `schedule_method of trade 1001 at row 1 is 'penultimate', expected 'asian' or 'lastday' on a published index; a published month is the average of its prints, or its one print`. With two indexes a published leg takes `lastday` alone, the second leg's rules below. The index was made with a reporting lot since #164, the specification the batch's delta is counted in lots of, since a batch without one could not report its risk, `index 'ETHANE' has no reporting_lot, expected one of its specifications named when the index was made; a batch's delta is reported in lots of it`; one or more occurrences, a date each, a specification each, a row of the index's specifications since the lot size and the lot unit are read there, whatever the desk's word for it, an exchange lot or a bare unit the index defines with a size of one, a method and a basis each, positions among their members, the three each row's own since #163, and, where given, a trade ID each, whole numbers of at most 15 digits the resolution read since #168, and for each label tuple given a text each; one or more components, each of an occurrence, every occurrence holding at least one, each contract a row of the schedule, each component covering the days from its start date to its end date, the end on or after the start and, under `monthly`, both in one calendar month, a `onetime` row's one component covering its whole window since [onetime-averaging.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/onetime-averaging.md), the days counted here, each quantity by its settlement's rule, `settled_quantities`, any finite number under a financial settlement and a whole number under a physical one, since [fractional-quantities.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/fractional-quantities.md), zero included under either, since a row kept ready for a deal holds a zero and contributes nothing, each fixed price finite, the quantity and the price named by the trade they belong to, as the sheet counts its rows. A financial settlement has payment terms, a lag and a calendar per occurrence and one payment day per component; a physical one has none, since it pays on no day and terms given would invent a payment. A penultimate, last3day or asian row settles financially, a physical batch taking lastday rows alone and the first other row named with its method, `settlement_method is 'physical', expected 'financial' with the penultimate method of trade 2 at row 2; a penultimate future is cash settled on the business day before its contract's expiry`. Each component's quantity is formed once by its occurrence's basis, since #159 and row by row since #163: the entered quantity under `month` and, since onetime-averaging.md, under `period`, the window's total, the frozen column shared when no row chose `calday`, and the entered quantity times its covered days under `calday`, a window's whole days included, its product looked at for finiteness, the overflow handled where it happens since a bound on the entered quantity is a rounded quotient a quantity at it can pass, `quantity of trade 1 at row 1 is 1e+308 over 31 days, expected a finite quantity`; the encoding converts it and nothing later scales again. The instrument is kept as the definition the terms stand under and read for nothing here. The quantity and the price rules, `settled_quantities` with the `finite_quantities` and `whole_quantities` it chooses between and `finite_prices`, are callable on their own, and the template reads its numbers through them.
