# The trade kernel

Built in #136 as ticket I1 of the [trade-valuation pipeline](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/README.md). Since #139, ticket I4, [_CreateTrades](create-trades.md) resolves the terms from a template and a ticket on the sheet and publishes the batch, the terms and the encoding together; the record gained the instrument, the clearing house and broker labels and the payment terms, and a quantity or a price is refused naming the trade's own row, which a strip's component row is not. Since #140, ticket I4a, the kernel's axis is the dated slot, [dated-exposure.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/dated-exposure.md): an observation day within a payment group, with the contract pricing that day as its reference, so that an observation fixing on its own day is set while the rest of the batch stays live; a `lastday` future has one slot per contract, its expiry day, and every number of the NG example stands. Since #141, ticket I5, [_PrepareTrades](prepare-trades.md), [_EvaluateTrades](evaluate-trades.md), [_ReportValues](report-values.md) and [_ReportRisk](report-risk.md) stand on it, the records `PreparedTrades` and `TradeResults` named for the handle words they publish under. Since #146, [zero-quantities.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/zero-quantities.md), a quantity of zero is a whole count like any other, a row kept ready for a deal, carried through every step and contributing nothing, its contract on the axis at a delta of zero. Since #162, [multiple-payment-groups.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/multiple-payment-groups.md), a payment group is a contract and the day it pays: a contract whose components pay on two days is two groups, each moving from mark to cash on its own day, while the delta axis holds the contract once and a tick reads its quote once. Since #163, [mixed-trade-fields.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/mixed-trade-fields.md), each occurrence has its own specification, schedule method and quantity basis, so one batch mixes units, methods and bases row by row and values through the one path.

The four steps of the pipeline as library code, `src/goosedove/trades/`: the resolved terms of a batch and their observations, their static encoding on dated slots, the batch prepared against one market history, and its evaluation against one market. One occurrence takes the same path as a thousand and every occurrence counts, a duplicate twice. The [NG example](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/ng-creation-preparation-example.md) is the acceptance fixture: every hand answer of mark, cash and delta on that page, the real NG rows before expiry and the toy lifecycle through its seven states, is pinned in `tests/unit/test_trades.py`, the page's per-group amounts at historical prices pinned only as each state's set total, with the daily toy and the offsetting dates of the dated-exposure page, and the work of each step in `tests/performance/test_trade_budgets.py`.

Each record keeps the one before it by reference, so a result explains itself back to the terms and the index, and every array is owned and read-only. Nothing in the kernel imports the Excel bridge; the unit tables and the work counter arrive as arguments and are never worksheet parameters. The fixing source is one for the batch, the index and the history over it, kept once and repeated in no slot.

## What each step does, and refuses

**The terms.** A futures index or, since #167, an IFERC index, [iferc-basis-futures.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/iferc-basis-futures.md), since a fixed_float's contract is a row of a futures schedule, its final price the settlement on that row's expiry day, or a row of an IFERC schedule, its final price the month's index on that row's publication day, the traded basis, a Gas Daily index refused by name; an IFERC index takes a financial settlement and `lastday` rows alone, an IFERC month having one index, published once, the first row of another method named, `settlement_method is 'physical', expected 'financial' on an IFERC index; an IFERC basis future is cash settled on its month's published index`, `schedule_method of trade 1001 at row 1 is 'asian', expected 'lastday' on an IFERC index; an IFERC month has one index, published once`; made with a reporting lot since #164, the specification the batch's delta is counted in lots of, since a batch without one could not report its risk, `index 'ETHANE' has no reporting_lot, expected one of its specifications named when the index was made; a batch's delta is reported in lots of it`; one or more occurrences, a date each, a specification each, a row of the index's specifications since the lot size and the lot unit are read there, whatever the desk's word for it, an exchange lot or a bare unit the index defines with a size of one, a method and a basis each, positions among their members, the three each row's own since #163, and, where given, a trade ID each, whole numbers of at most 15 digits the resolution read since #168, and for each label tuple given a text each; one or more components, each of an occurrence, every occurrence holding at least one, each contract a row of the schedule, each component covering the days from its start date to its end date, the end on or after the start and both in one calendar month, the days counted here, each quantity a whole number, zero included, since a row kept ready for a deal holds a zero and contributes nothing, each fixed price finite, the quantity and the price named by the trade they belong to, as the sheet counts its rows. A financial settlement has payment terms, a lag and a calendar per occurrence and one payment day per component; a physical one has none, since it pays on no day and terms given would invent a payment. A penultimate, last3day or asian row settles financially, a physical batch taking lastday rows alone and the first other row named with its method, `settlement_method is 'physical', expected 'financial' with the penultimate method of trade 2 at row 2; a penultimate future is cash settled on the business day before its contract's expiry`. Each component's quantity is formed once by its occurrence's basis, since #159 and row by row since #163: the entered quantity under `month`, the frozen column shared when no row chose `calday`, and the entered quantity times its covered days under `calday`, its product looked at for finiteness, the overflow handled where it happens since a bound on the entered quantity is a rounded quotient a quantity at it can pass, `quantity of trade 1 at row 1 is 1e+308 over 31 days, expected a finite quantity`; the encoding converts it and nothing later scales again. The instrument is kept as the definition the terms stand under and read for nothing here. The quantity and the price rules, `whole_quantities` and `finite_prices`, are callable on their own, and the template reads its numbers through them.

**The encoding.** The quote quantity is the component quantity, the count the terms formed by its occurrence's basis, times its occurrence's specification's lot size, restated in the conventions' quote quantity unit through the unit tables, both units of the quantity type as the index checked, the lot size and the factor formed once per distinct specification the batch selects since #163 and gathered onto the components. The coefficient is the quote quantity restated from the quote currency to the settlement currency, so that a price in cents times it is an amount in dollars; every price the batch meets, a quote, a settle or the fixed price, multiplies the coefficient and never the quote quantity, which is kept apart as the physical fact. The offset is minus the coefficient times the fixed price. The groups are, under a financial settlement, the distinct pairs of contract and payment day among the components, in the schedule's order and by day within a contract, one lexsort, and under a physical settlement, which pays on no day, the distinct contracts; the offsets are summed by group, so two occurrences on one contract paying on one day are one group with their offsets added, a duplicate twice, and two paying on different days are two groups, each to move from mark to cash on its own day, the grouping by the day itself whatever lag and calendar made it, since #162. Each observation's day is one the index's market is open on, Monday to Friday less the index's holidays, by the calendar's rule, `open_days`, else refused: no settle exists for a closed day or ever will, and a live observation on one would take a quote today and fail the day it fixed, which the review of #140 caught. Each observation's reference is the contract pricing its day by the calendar's rule, `contract_on`, the first whose expiry is on or after it, one search over the schedule. Under the expiry-based methods it is the component's own contract, else the observation is refused by its method, since those methods take the contract's own days and a futures history holds one settle a day, the pricing contract's, never Z26's on a day X26 prices; under `asian`, since #165, it is whichever contract the calendar gives, F27 and then G27 for a December booked as Z26 on a schedule whose contracts expire the month before, the booked contract keeping the fixed leg and the payment and the day's contract supplying its settle and its quote, [calendar-driven-asian-references.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/calendar-driven-asian-references.md). The slots are the distinct days within each group, one lexsort, each observation's coefficient times its weight summed into its slot, one bincount, so two occurrences observing one day in one group are one slot with their exposures added, and one day observed by two groups of one contract is two slots. The delta axis is the distinct references, each contract once whatever groups hold it, the contracts that price the batch's days and not the ones it was booked on. On an IFERC index, since #167, each observation's reference is its component's own month, whose index publishes on that very day, else refused, an IFERC month pricing once, and no calendar is read, an IFERC index having none. For a financial settlement each group pays after its last observation. The fees of the specification are read once, since #170, for the batch's reserves: each component's count without its sign times its occurrence's specification's brokerage and clearing rates per lot, the rates as given, summed for the batch, since #175 the marketing reserve the rows booked summed once each, the amounts not kept and the totals never a contribution to value, [report-reserves.md](report-reserves.md). ## The bilateral path

Since #176, [bilateral-calculation-kernel.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/bilateral-calculation-kernel.md), UAT's bilateral calculation slice, the one evaluator values a bilateral batch on a discount curve, `trade_results(prepared, market, counter, curve)`, the curve an optional fourth argument the worksheet function does not take yet. The rules above come first; then the live contributions are summed onto the payment groups, one bincount, and each group's amount is its entry-price offset, its set amount and its live amount together, so a group is discounted whole, the fixed and the live legs alike, before any two payment days are added. The unpaid groups, their distinct payment days, each group's place among those days and its time fraction are the preparation's, mapped once when the batch is prepared, since the groups settled and the valuation date change only then, [prepare-trades.md](prepare-trades.md); the curve's factor is read once per distinct unpaid payment day, by position, the curve being daily from its start, and placed on the groups through that mapping, so a tick sorts and counts no day; a paid group is cash already, reads no factor and holds the policy factor of one. Each unpaid group's NPV is its amount times its factor and the mark is their sum. DV01 is the analytic signed sensitivity of that mark to a rise of one basis point in the continuously compounded zero rates, ACT/365F: `-0.0001 * tau * D * amount` per unpaid group, `tau` the calendar days from the valuation date to the payment day over 365, summed, negative for an amount receivable and positive for one payable; no bump, no re-interpolation of the anchors, no second evaluation. The discounted lots are each slot's lots times its group's factor, a fixed slot's lots being zero, before any sum, since two slots of one day and one contract may pay on different days; summed over every slot onto the references, the days, the months and the years exactly as the preparation sums the nominal lots, one path whether any slot is live, since numpy's sum over no slot is integer zeros and not a sum of lots; and the discounted sensitivity is the lots times the reporting lot's coefficient. Every stored vector is checked before the result is made: each group's amount, paid or not, the mark and the DV01, the discounted lots by reference, the discounted sensitivity by reference, the three views and the lots' total, the first past the largest number refused by its group, the batch, its contract or its bucket; the arithmetic runs with numpy's overflow flag down, so the rule and not a warning is the judge; the cash is the preparation's and is checked there. A cleared batch is as it was, its discounted vectors the nominal arrays by reference and its group vectors none.

```text
amount[g]  = offset_of_group[g] + set_amount[g] + sum(exposure[s] * quote[reference(s)], live slots of g)
D[g]       = curve.factors[days - curve.start_date][place[g]]       days and place the preparation's; 1 for a paid group
tau[g]     = (payment_date[g] - as_of) / 365
npv[g]     = amount[g] * D[g]                                       0 for a paid group
dv01[g]    = -0.0001 * tau[g] * npv[g]
mark       = sum(npv);  dv01 = sum(dv01);  cash = the preparation's
lots[s]    = dated_live_lots[s] * D[group(s)]                       every slot, zero where fixed; summed by reference, day, month, year
```
