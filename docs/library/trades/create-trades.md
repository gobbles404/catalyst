# _CreateTrades

Built in #139 as ticket I4 of the [trade-valuation pipeline](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/README.md), with [_CreateTradeTemplate](trade-template.md); the design is [CreateTrades: resolution, encoding and retained state](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/create-trade.md).

A batch of trades on one index: the deals a ticket's rows describe, one trade per row, completed with the shared fields a template supplies, resolved against the index and encoded into the static arrays the [kernel](kernel.md) prices. One row is a batch of one and a repeated row is a second trade; the batch is immutable, retains no history, price or valuation date, and is what `_PrepareTrades` takes. The code is `src/goosedove/trades/resolution.py`, the resolution child and the batch, `src/goosedove/trades/template.py`, the readers of each field's value, and `src/goosedove/excel/trades.py`, the worksheet function.

## Worksheet contract

def _CreateTrades(
    index: Handle[Index],
    fields: TextRow,
    trade_rows: FieldRows[TradeTemplate],
    template: Handle[TradeTemplate],
) -> Handle[Trades]: ...
```

Four arguments, all required, none with a default. The index is the first: the cell showing `_CreateIndex`'s result, or a name for it, the one index every trade of the batch is on; it is no template field and no ticket column, so one template serves any compatible index, and the batch retains the index it was resolved against. The ticket is two arguments, as a table is: the header row, each header a field name shown by `_ShowInstrument` spelled exactly, case and all, in any order, and the rows under it, one trade each. The template is last: the cell showing `_CreateTradeTemplate`'s result, or a name for it. It selects the instrument the headers are checked against and supplies the fields the ticket does not; there is no path without one, so a batch that shares nothing still names one shared fact in its template. The ticket crosses as `FieldRows[TradeTemplate]`, the seam's row-or-rows form of [inputs.md](../../seam/inputs.md) decision 42, bound on the call against the template's instrument: the seam resolves the two handles, checks the width, the headers and each cell by its field's type, and hands the function typed columns; nothing here restates that. The result is a handle, text standing for the batch the seam keeps, `30512:trades:Henry Hub NYMEX:41`, the index's name between the kind and the count, as [handles.md](../../seam/handles.md) says. A successful call publishes a batch and changes neither input; a recalculation with the same arguments shares the live batch, and a changed cell anywhere under it, a ticket value, the template, the index, makes a new one, the old released when Excel disconnects its last creator.

## What resolution does

Codes become schedule rows by month. Every code of the index's schedule is read once for its month, `(year - 1970) * 12 + month - 1` as numpy counts months, through the one reader of a contract code, `contract_codes.month_and_year`; each distinct text of the ticket's `contract_code` column is read once through `periods.period_months`, the grammar behind `util_StartDate`, for its first month and the month after its last, so `CAL27` is twelve months and `Z27-X28` twelve from December 2027, since #172 among the rows that book by code alone; then one pass of array arithmetic makes the components, one per month of each row in order, all on that row's occurrence, the coded and the dated rows' components in row order together, and looks each month up in the schedule's months, a month without a contract refused by the row and the month. The mapping from component to schedule row is made here once and retained in the terms; preparation and ticks never redo it, and the schedule's codes stay the labels the desk typed, `u26` on the spill where the ticket typed `U26`. A template's `contract_code` puts every row without dates on the same contracts, and is read only when some row books by it, so a template holds a label the grammar refuses until a row does.

The quantity unit is each row's own selection since #163, [mixed-trade-fields.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/mixed-trade-fields.md), the template's value for every row or the ticket's column: a name among the index's specifications, the desk's word for a quantity of the index, `NYMEX NG` for the 10,000 MMBtu lot, `ICE PHH` for ICE's 2,500, `MMBTU` where the index defines a specification of that name with a size of one; the row it names supplies the lot size and the lot unit the encoding converts by, once per distinct selection the batch makes, and nothing later looks the name up again. Quantities and prices are the row's, repeated over the months of a strip, each quantity a signed whole count of the selected specification, zero for a row kept ready for a deal, since #146; `quantity_per` is `month` or `calday`, each row's own since #163 and one for the batch from #159 to then: under `month` the entered quantity stands in every constituent month, a partial month's as much as a whole one's, unprorated; under `calday` it is per calendar day, weekends and holidays included, and each month's quantity is the entered quantity times the days it covers, the whole month here, 31 for December, 28 or 29 for February, so 100 a day over `X26-Z26` is 3,000 and 3,100 and 110 a day over Z26 is 3,410. The resolution counts the days off the contract's month, a December contract expiring in November covering December's 31, and no pricing or payment calendar moves them; the kernel forms each component's quantity once, the encoding converts it, and nothing later, a tick least of all, recounts or scales again. `trade_id` is the source system's deal number since #168, a whole number of at most 15 digits, the template's one repeated or the ticket's column, read once for the rule and held as an int64, duplicates kept as separate trades, [numeric-trade-ids.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/numeric-trade-ids.md); `clearing_house` and `broker` are labels kept as typed, one per row, the template's repeated, read for nothing. `trade_date` is the row's or the template's.

The other period form, since #161: `start_date` and `end_date`, a column each or a template value each, and since #172 beside `contract_code` on one ticket, [contract-codes-and-dates.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/contract-codes-and-dates.md): each row chooses, a complete pair booking the days between them whatever the code beside it says, the code kept as entered text and read for nothing, so `BALV26` or a code naming another month beside a pair stands and moves nothing; a row without a pair books its code; a blank cell or a formula's `""` in the three fields supplies nothing, the header still the template's or the ticket's to own; a pair may be split, the template's start with the ticket's end, and is complete or absent on each row, half a pair refused by its row. The resolution splits the period at month ends into components, each bound to its contract by month as a code's months are and each covering its month's days but the first from the start date and the last to the end date, so 12 November to 31 December is X26 over 12 to 30 November, 19 days, and Z26 over the whole of December, 31; a whole month by dates is the code's own components, observations and encoding, and the readback shows the code the resolution bound. Under `asian` a component observes the pricing days of the days it covers, an equal share each, and pays from the last of them, so shortening the end can move the payment day; under `lastday`, `penultimate` and `last3day` a component must cover its whole month, since their observations are the contract's expiry days, which a part of a month has no share of. [util_StartDate](../utilities/start-date.md) and [util_EndDate](../utilities/end-date.md) give both days from a label, `BALZ26` with the valuation-date handle since #173 and `Z26` with or without one, so a column of ordinary and `BAL` labels feeds one dated ticket, the days pasted as values before booking so that the trade stands where the handle moves on; the library receives dates and no label, and a `BAL` label in `contract_code` is refused as any other word where a row books by it. Two rows on one contract from different starts to one end share a batch and a payment day; different ends may give different payment days, and since #162 those rows share a batch too, each in a payment group of its own, [multiple-payment-groups.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/multiple-payment-groups.md).

Each row's schedule method gives its components their observation days since #163, one for the batch from #144 to then, through the kernel's `observed`, made once here, the methods present made once each and the observations put back in component order and by day when the batch mixes them: `lastday`, the contract's expiry day, the schedule's `future_expiry`, or on an IFERC index since #167 the month's publication day, the schedule's `publication_date`, the one method an IFERC index takes; `penultimate`, since #144, the business day before it, Monday to Friday less the index's holiday calendar, one `numpy.busday_offset` of -1 over the components, an expiry the index's market is closed on refused by its contract rather than rolled, `Z26 expires on 2026-11-26, a day the index's market is closed; the penultimate method takes the business day before an expiry the market prices`; `last3day`, since #157, the expiry day and the two business days before it, a third of the quantity each, one `numpy.busday_offset` over the components and the three offsets at once, a closed expiry refused the same way, `Z26 expires on 2026-12-04, a day the index's market is closed; the last3day method takes an expiry the market prices and the two business days before it`, and a day among the three that another contract prices refused by the encoding, since no settle of the component's contract exists for it, [last3day-trades.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/last3day-trades.md); `asian`, since #158, every Monday to Friday of the days the component covers the index's market is open on, an equal share each, its contract's whole month from a code and the booked part of it from dates since #161, the days the resolution hands over so that no code is read again, one `numpy.busday_count` over the components and one `numpy.busday_offset` over the observations, a component with no pricing day refused by its contract, its trade and its days, `G27 of trade 1 at row 1 has no pricing day from 2027-02-01 to 2027-02-28, expected a Monday to Friday of the days it covers the index's market is open on; the asian method averages the pricing days a component covers`, a closed month-end simply no observation, and a day another contract prices taken off that contract since #165, the booked contract keeping the fixed leg and the payment, [asian-trades.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/asian-trades.md) and [calendar-driven-asian-references.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/calendar-driven-asian-references.md). The underlying schedule is unchanged: ICE PHH on the NYMEX NG index observes Tuesday 24 November 2026 for Z26, the day before NYMEX's Wednesday, and ICE WTI T on a CL index Friday 18 December for F27, ICE's own published last trading days; a penultimate future is cash settled, so the kernel refuses it with a physical settlement.

For a financial settlement each component pays its row's rule applied to its anchor instruction by instruction, since #NN, `+2bd` two business days after it, `+3em+25cd-1bd+1bd` the end of the month three ahead, 25 calendar days on, the business day before and the one after, [compound-date-offsets.md](../../engineering-tickets/trade-valuation-pipeline/compound-date-offsets.md); the anchor is on a futures index its last observation day, the one day under `lastday` and `penultimate`, the expiry under `last3day` and the month's last pricing day under `asian`, and on an IFERC index since #167 its month's last trading day, the schedule's `last_trading_date`, the index printing later, so that WAH001's `+3bd` counts from 30 November to 3 December while Z26's index prints on 1 December, counted on its row's payment calendar with Monday to Friday eligible less the calendar's holidays, through `numpy.busday_offset` with the day rolled back to the last eligible day on or before it first, so that a day the payment calendar closes is not itself counted, calendar days and month ends whatever the calendar, and no roll at the end, so a rule ending in `cd` or `em` may pay on a closed day, `+1cd` from a Friday the Saturday, while the kernel holds the final day after the group's last observation as it always has, `+1bd-1bd` coming back to the anchor and refused there: a Friday expiry with the Monday closed pays Tuesday at `+1bd` and Wednesday at `+2bd`, and Thanksgiving Thursday pays Friday at `+1bd`; from Friday 9 October 2026 `+1bd` and `+3cd` both pay Monday the 12th and are one group, `+4cd` the Tuesday and a group of its own, `+0em-1bd+1bd` Monday 2 November. The toy of the NG example, `+2bd` on a calendar closed on 26 November and 25 December 2026, pays Z26 on 30 November and F27 on 31 December, the page's dates; PHH N27, `+1bd` from its pen day, Friday 25 June 2027, pays Monday the 28th, the day the underlying expires, and a pen day the clearing calendar closes pays the first clearing day after it, never the second. Each distinct rule text is read once and applied once per calendar it is paid on, to every component of that rule and calendar at once, instruction by instruction as arrays, the counter saying `payment_rules_read` and `payment_shifts`; the rule is kept as written and read back under `payment_time_lag` beside the day it made. A physical settlement pays on no day and supplies neither field. On a futures index the anchor is the component's last observation day and nothing else, the rule of the four methods, found by one search over the observations in component order, however many a method gives; on an IFERC index, since #167, it is the month's last trading date, the schedule's own column, the index printing after the exchange stops trading the month, so the observation day, the publication day, is never the anchor there. No field is added to the schedule for either, no anchor is kept beside the observations, and a later convention, a bilateral rate among them, states its own payment rule on its own page.

Then the kernel: `resolved_trades` checks the first profile's rules and freezes the columns, `encoded` turns them into quantities, coefficients, offsets and the inputs the batch consumes and, since #170, books the batch's reserves, the brokerage and the clearing its rows incur at their specifications' rates, [report-reserves.md](report-reserves.md), as [kernel.md](kernel.md) says, and the batch holds both, the encoding holding the terms by reference as well. Nothing is read of a history, a market, a price or a valuation date.

               gives it and its fixed price; for a financial settlement the payment terms, per
               row the lag in business days and
               the calendar it is counted on, per component the day they give

               offset; the payment groups, a contract and the day it pays, and the dated slots
    code_entered   per row, since #172, whether it entered a contract_code, a read-only mask
    entered_codes  the texts of the rows that did, in row order, as typed, apart from the contracts
```

Every array is owned and read-only; the index, the schedule, the payment calendars and the instrument definition are held by reference, never copied. The payment terms travel with the days they gave, so a batch explains its own payment days without the template or the ticket; the template and the raw ticket themselves are not retained. The fees on the specification are read by nothing: reserves, never a contribution to value. The handle's text names the index, `30512:trades:Henry Hub NYMEX:41`, and the batch retains it as an object.

## Reading it back

`util_ReadHandle` on a trades handle spills one row per component, the resolved terms under the definition's own field names in the definition's order and then what the encoding made of them: `row`, the ticket's row the component belongs to, as the sheet counts rows; `trade_id` where the batch has it, a number since #168; `trade_date`; `contract_code`, the schedule's spelling; `schedule_method`, `route` and `settlement_method`; `clearing_house`, since #175 `counterparty`, and `broker` where the batch has them; `quantity`, `quantity_unit`, the specification's name as the index spells it, and `quantity_per`; `fixed_price`; for a financial settlement `payment_time_lag`, `+2bd`, and `payment_date`, the day it gave; since #175 `marketing_reserve` where the batch has it, the row's one amount on each of its components and no allocation among them, never summed down a strip; then what the resolution derived, since #172 `entered_contract_code`, the code text the row entered as typed and `(not supplied)` where it entered none, display words and no value, apart from `contract_code`, the contract the resolution bound, then since #159 and #161 `start_date` and `end_date`, the first and the last day the component covers, its whole month from a code and its share of a booked period from dates, `covered_days`, the calendar days between them, and `component_quantity`, the quantity the basis gives it, the entered quantity times those days under `calday` and the entered quantity under `month`, so that the month's total is never read as the entered daily quantity; then `quote_quantity`, the component quantity in the index's quote quantity unit, and `coefficient` and `offset` in the settlement currency. The batch's facts stand on every row, since a row should read as the whole deal and the template need not have supplied them: a route or a settlement that came from the ticket's columns is read back here and nowhere else. The NG example spills seven rows by twenty columns, the five components after the two heading rows, `100000` and `-310000` last on the first; the toy's strip `Z26-F27` spills two components on row 1 with `+2bd` and their two payment days. On a template handle the reader spills one row, the instrument first and each default under its field name, a holiday calendar as the words of its kind. Neither spill is a round trip of a handle.
